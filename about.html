About AniNextUp

Get to know AniNextUp: who publishes our anime guides, how we research release dates and streaming options, and what to check before you watch.

AniNextUp is an independent English-language guide to anime release dates, streaming availability and upcoming announcements. We help viewers answer practical questions: What can I watch next? When does a series begin? Which service has announced it? Is that date for Japan, an international release or an English dub? Our purpose is to make those distinctions easier to understand before you plan your viewing.

Anime information often arrives in pieces. A teaser may confirm a year before a broadcaster reveals a day, while an overseas streaming announcement comes later. We bring those pieces together in release guides, news coverage and schedules, explaining both what is known and what still needs confirmation.

Who is behind AniNextUp?

Our articles and release guides appear under the name AniNextUp Editorial Team. That is the editorial identity responsible for the site's coverage. AniNextUp is an independent project; our name and byline do not imply that we represent an anime studio, publisher, production committee or streaming platform.

We focus on the information readers need to make viewing decisions. Rather than asking you to interpret a promotional announcement on your own, a useful guide should explain its scope, provide relevant context and identify the questions it does not answer. You can read our Editorial Policy for the standards behind sourcing, updates and corrections.

What can you find on the site?

Our News section covers announcements and developments such as new anime adaptations, returning seasons, promotional videos and streaming plans. Individual articles explain the announcement and its implications for viewers. When a subject needs more background, a guide can address watch order, different adaptations, language options or regional availability.

The Calendar helps you look ahead at upcoming releases. Today and This Week organize the schedule over shorter periods. Check the coverage notes on those pages: a selected regional schedule is not a complete list of every anime airing around the world. Our Where to Watch hub provides another route into articles organized around streaming services.

How do we research anime release information?

We prioritize announcements from official anime websites, production committees, studios, publishers, distributors and streaming services. These sources have different responsibilities. A Japanese broadcaster can establish a domestic television slot, for example, while an international distributor is better placed to confirm its own streaming territories.

We consider what a source actually establishes before describing a detail as confirmed. An announcement of a television adaptation does not automatically establish an episode count. A platform logo on promotional material does not necessarily explain language options or worldwide access. Where the available announcement leaves a gap, the article should explain that gap rather than fill it with an assumption.

Source references support the reporting and let readers trace important claims. Our articles add organization, explanations and viewing context around those announcements. References are evidence for the information we discuss; AniNextUp remains responsible for how that information is presented on its own pages.

What do “confirmed,” “TBA” and release windows mean?

Confirmed means that an identifiable announcement supports the particular detail being described. The scope matters: a confirmed Japanese premiere is not automatically a confirmed premiere in your country. We aim to specify which release an announced date concerns.

TBA means “to be announced.” It signals that a detail has not been established in the information available for the guide. A release window, such as a season or a month, is less specific than an exact date. If only a year has been announced, presenting an invented month would make a guide less useful, even if that estimate later happened to be right.

Rumors, fan predictions and production speculation should not become confirmed release information. An absent detail also does not prove a cancellation. Sometimes the accurate answer is simply that the next announcement has not arrived.

Why might a release date or time look different?

Several dates can apply to the same anime: its Japanese broadcast, a theatrical opening, an international simulcast, a dubbed release or the arrival of older episodes on another service. A guide should make clear which event its date describes. The publication date below an article's introduction describes the article itself.

Time zones can also move an episode onto a different calendar day. Japanese television schedules sometimes use late-night notation that extends beyond midnight. Meanwhile, a regional streaming service may publish its own local schedule. Read the stated region and time zone before treating a listed time as your local release time.

Our schedule pages identify the coverage they support. A weekly broadcast slot does not establish an episode number unless that number is also known. Delays, breaks and revised distribution plans can change a schedule, so check for meaningful updates when planning around a particular release.

Does “Where to Watch” mean an anime is available everywhere?

No. Streaming rights vary by country, service, season and language. A platform may carry the original series in one territory while another service has a newer season elsewhere. A dubbed version can arrive separately from the subtitled release, and a catalog listing may cover only part of a long-running series.

Our streaming guides explain the territories and conditions that available announcements support. When a region has not been verified, we should say so. Before purchasing a subscription for a specific title, check the service's local listing for the season, episodes and audio options you want. AniNextUp does not control platform catalogs, subscription terms or playback access.

Can you watch full anime episodes on AniNextUp?

AniNextUp is a guide and news site, not a full-episode streaming service. We help you identify viewing options and understand release information. We do not provide an AniNextUp subscription that unlocks episodes from other companies, and we cannot resolve account or billing issues for those services.

Official promotional videos may appear within our articles. A trailer is a preview, not proof that the full series is already available. Its images can also represent a particular season, film or adaptation, so the surrounding explanation matters.

How do we choose trailers and images?

We aim to include an official trailer when a relevant, identifiable official upload is available. That means checking the video and its source rather than choosing a reupload solely because its title matches the anime. If a suitable official video is unavailable, leaving it out is more accurate than substituting unrelated footage.

We prefer official promotional artwork and key visuals with relevant attribution. Promotional images and embedded videos belong to their respective rights holders. Their appearance on AniNextUp does not imply a partnership or endorsement. An upload can later become private, unavailable or restricted by region; readers can report a broken embed for review.

How are articles updated and corrected?

A release guide can change as new information becomes available. An exact date may replace a release window, a distributor may announce additional territories, or an official delay may make an older schedule inaccurate. Meaningful editorial updates should reflect those developments without disguising the article's original publication date.

Changing navigation, styling or related article links is not the same as checking every factual claim again. A visible publication date tells you when the article was published; it does not promise that every streaming catalog was inspected today. Look at the wording of the relevant section and any update information when assessing a time-sensitive detail.

If you notice an error, use our Contact page. Include the article address, the statement that needs attention and, if possible, an official announcement supporting the correction. Those details help us distinguish a reporting error from a regional difference or a newly changed schedule. We cannot promise an immediate response or a particular publication outcome.

How can you get the most from AniNextUp?

Start with a question. Use the schedule pages when you want to know what is coming up, the streaming hub when you are choosing where to watch, or search for a specific series when you need its release guide. Related articles can help you distinguish a sequel, remake, film or dubbed edition with a similar name.
