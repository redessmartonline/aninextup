What makes the premise distinctive?

Rather than introducing a single monster as the central mystery, HORROR COLLECTOR builds its premise around rumors that become tangible. Fushigi’s search provides a continuing thread, while each new legend can raise a different question: who started the rumor, why did it spread, and what happens when people encounter it? This is an interpretation of the announced premise, not a claim about scenes or storylines that have yet to be confirmed.

What to check before watching

The announced 12-episode run describes the season length; it does not tell viewers how many legends will appear in each installment. Likewise, the October 10 date is the Japanese NHK television premiere, not a universal streaming release time. For a practical viewing plan, check the episode listing in your own Crunchyroll region and distinguish the Japanese broadcast schedule from the platform’s local availability.

When does HORROR COLLECTOR premiere?

HORROR COLLECTOR premieres October 10, 2026. The Japanese television broadcast begins on NHK General TV, bringing the children's horror novel series to television animation.

Where to watch

HORROR COLLECTOR is included in Crunchyroll's Fall 2026 international lineup. Streaming availability and exact episode timing can vary by territory, while the Japanese broadcast is handled by NHK.

Japan streaming: The official anime website schedules Episode 1 for October 15, 2026, at 12:00 p.m. JST on Japanese services including ABEMA Premium, DMM TV, Hulu, U-NEXT, d Anime Store and FOD Premium. This is separate from the October 10 NHK television premiere and does not establish a Crunchyroll release time outside Japan. See the official Japanese streaming announcement.

How many episodes?

The first television season is confirmed for 12 episodes. That gives the adaptation a defined single-cour run for its first collection of supernatural stories.

What is HORROR COLLECTOR about?

Fushigi, a mysterious boy in a red hood, travels from place to place collecting urban legends that have taken physical form. He pursues Himitsu alongside his unusual partner Jimmy as the series explores frightening rumors and phenomena that may be much closer to everyday life than they first appear.

How to approach an urban-legend anthology

The published premise gives viewers two ways to follow the story: Fushigi's ongoing pursuit of Himitsu and the individual rumors that take physical form. Those are different kinds of narrative questions. The first concerns a continuing character goal; the second concerns the fear attached to a particular legend. This is a reading guide based on the synopsis, not confirmation that each of the 12 episodes will adapt exactly one legend.

Opening and ending themes

Japanese singer-songwriter Shikao Suga wrote and performs both the opening and ending themes. The official announcement notes his own interest in J-horror and describes the anime as a fusion of urban legends and Japanese horror.
