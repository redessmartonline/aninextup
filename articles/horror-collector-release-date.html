When did HORROR COLLECTOR premiere?

HORROR COLLECTOR began airing in Japan on October 10, 2026. The NHK General TV slot is Saturday at 11:45 p.m. Japan Standard Time (JST), and the official announcement confirms a 12-episode first season. The premiere date has passed; viewers should check their local service for the latest episode. Because the Japanese slot is close to midnight, other time zones may show a different calendar date. Use NHK's current listing to confirm a particular week's broadcast.

These are Japanese listings, not worldwide availability. The cited official notices do not confirm a platform or hour for every international territory. What is the anime about?

The official synopsis follows Fushigi Chino, a boy in a red hood who travels to collect urban legends that have taken physical form in a notebook. He is trying to catch Himitsu, a girl connected to the legends' manifestation, and travels with Jimmy, his human-faced dog partner. Each disturbance brings a supernatural mystery, while Fushigi's pursuit of Himitsu gives the story a continuing thread.

The premise centers on rumors and modern urban legends becoming real, rather than one haunted location or creature driving the whole season. That is based on the official synopsis; it does not confirm that each episode adapts one separate legend or follows a one-story-per-episode structure.

How many episodes are confirmed?

The first TV season is confirmed for 12 episodes. That establishes the season length, but not how many books, chapters or legends it covers. It also does not confirm a second season. Readers deciding whether to start the novels should treat the anime as a separate adaptation until the production identifies its endpoint. Who voices the main characters?

Hiiro Ishibashi voices Fushigi, Jun Fukuyama voices Jimmy, Saori Hayami voices Himitsu, and Nobuhiko Okamoto voices the Blue Umbrella Man, according to the official cast page. These roles match the central group introduced in the synopsis: the boy tracking the legends, his companion, the girl linked to them and a mysterious figure whose purpose is not fully explained there.

The official character pages provide Japanese names, cast credits and short descriptions. This guide sticks to roles on that official listing rather than combining them with guest cast announced for individual episodes.

Which studio and staff are making the series?

Studio Palette handles animation. Yuki Inaba directs and oversees series composition; Kaori Yoshikawa designs the characters; Masato Koda composes the music; and Tetsuya Gunji is sound director. The original work is credited to Midori Sato and Norio Tsuruta, with character concepts by Yon. NHK Enterprises is named in production, and NHK is credited alongside KADOKAWA. These credits confirm that the anime adapts an existing children's horror novel series rather than being described as an original project. What songs are in HORROR COLLECTOR?

Shikao Suga wrote and performs both the opening and ending themes, the official music announcement says. The staff page names the opening as “Kageboshi,” but does not list the ending title, so this guide leaves that title open. Suga also noted his interest in Japanese horror when discussing his work on both songs.

How does the anime connect to the novels?

KADOKAWA's Tsubasa Bunko publishes the source novels by Sato and Tsuruta, illustrated by Yon. Its page for volume 29, released October 7, 2026, describes it as the climax of Fushigi and Himitsu's pursuit. That shows the novels have a long publishing history, but it does not tell viewers where a 12-episode anime season will stop. The publisher catalogs the books for middle elementary readers. Families can use that category as a reference for the novels, then consult the anime synopsis and episodes to judge the animated version's scarier scenes for themselves.
