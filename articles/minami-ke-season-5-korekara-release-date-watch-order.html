Minami-ke Season 5 Release Date, Trailer & Watch Order

The fifth Minami-ke TV season, Minami-ke: Korekara, premieres in Japan in April 2027. The original voices of Haruka, Kana and Chiaki return. Here’s the official teaser, the order for the four earlier TV seasons and where the optional specials fit.

When does Minami-ke Season 5 come out?

Minami-ke: Korekara is scheduled to begin airing in Japan in April 2027. The announcement at the November 8, 2026 Minami-ke Special Event introduced the teaser visual, footage and returning cast alongside that release window. April is the confirmed month. The sources reviewed for this guide give no premiere day, broadcast time or episode count. If you are setting a reminder, use the month as a placeholder until the production publishes a schedule.

The series was first announced as a new anime in 2024, and the Korekara subtitle was revealed in 2025. The November 2026 announcement is the one that adds the April broadcast window. That sequence explains why older listings may show only an announcement, a subtitle or a broad release year. For the latest information, look for the full Japanese title みなみけ これから on the production site. It is the same project often listed in English as Minami-ke Season 5.

Minami-ke TV watch order before Korekara

For the main television series, watch the four seasons in their original release order. Each has its own title, which is more reliable than the season numbering in an app or database. This order starts with the sisters at home and lets their school friends and recurring jokes accumulate gradually before the fifth series.

  1. Minami-ke (2007) is the first TV series. Start here to meet Haruka, Kana and Chiaki, see how their household works and get used to the rhythm of a story that moves between home and school.
  2. Minami-ke: Okawari (2008) is the second TV series. Search for the subtitle Okawari when a service splits it from the original. The animation studio changed from Daume to asread, which helps explain the difference in presentation when you move straight from one to the other.
  3. Minami-ke: Okaeri (2009) is the third TV series. The subtitle resembles Okawari at a glance, so check both the spelling and the year before starting. Asread also worked on this season.
  4. Minami-ke: Tadaima (2013) is the fourth TV series and the last TV entry before Korekara. Studio feel. animated it. This is the stop to return to if you already remember the early seasons and want the most recent television material before the revival.
  5. Minami-ke: Korekara (April 2027) follows as the fifth television entry. Its separate subtitle continues the naming pattern of the previous seasons.

If you have limited time before April, start with the 2007 series and carry on from there. Returning viewers can pick up from the TV subtitle they remember last. The show is built around household routines and recurring characters, so you can make the catch-up manageable by finishing one season at a time instead of trying to locate every special before starting the next TV entry.

Where do the Minami-ke OVAs fit?

There are three earlier original video animations: Betsubara, Omatase and Natsuyasumi. If you want a release-order marathon, put Betsubara after Okaeri, then Omatase before Tadaima, and Natsuyasumi after Tadaima. The fourth television season still means Tadaima when a streaming catalog groups the extras differently.

Treat the three specials as optional for the TV catch-up. The practical distinction is whether you want the four-season route or the additional stories released in between. If you are making a checklist, keep the specials under their own titles and use the TV list above as the backbone. That avoids accidentally counting a one-off as an entire season and ending up one subtitle ahead of where you intended to start.

Watch the official Minami-ke: Korekara teaser

The teaser goes back to the Minami apartment. Haruka calls the others for a meal, and the familiar friction between Kana and Chiaki brings the sisters’ dynamic back into focus. Friends from school and the other Minami family also appear. The official production announcement points out that the release month appears at the end, so the footage doubles as the first broadcast-window reveal.

Watch the official teaser on YouTube

If you are returning after a long break, the teaser is a useful way to hear the voices before revisiting the old seasons. If you are new, the most revealing moments are the domestic ones. A family meal and a quarrel give a better sense of the show's scale than a synopsis that promises a big adventure. The comedy often comes from how differently the sisters react to the same ordinary event.

Who is returning for Season 5?

Rina Sato returns as Haruka, Marina Inoue as Kana and Minori Chihara as Chiaki. Haruka is the eldest and tends to keep the household steady. Kana is the middle sister whose energy often starts an argument or an experiment, and Chiaki is the youngest and has a sharp reply ready. Remembering those roles is enough to follow the basic dynamic even if you have forgotten the supporting cast.

The official announcement also lists Nana Mizuki as Toma Minami, Rika Morinaga as Makoto, Tetsuya Kakihara as Fujioka and Daisuke Ono as Hosaka. Toma belongs to the other Minami family, a useful distinction when reading character lists where the surname repeats. Junichi Kitamura directs the new series at LIDENFILMS, with Takamitsu Kono handling series composition, Nobuhiro Ando designing the characters and Yasuhiro Misawa composing the music.

Where can you watch Minami-ke?

The April 2027 announcement applies to the new Japanese television anime. The official announcement reviewed for this guide does not list an international streaming platform, an English dub or a subtitle schedule for Korekara. Treat an older season’s streaming link and the YouTube teaser as separate references. Neither gives you an episode page for Season 5.

For viewers in Japan, d Anime Store lists the original Minami-ke and Tadaima, with related entries for the other TV seasons and OVAs. Those are Japanese catalog listings. If you are watching from another region, search by the subtitle of the season you need and check whether the service actually offers playback and the language options you want. A historical streaming announcement only tells you which service carried it at that time.

The source manga by Koharu Sakuraba is also available to sample in Japanese through Kodansha’s Young Magazine site. The publisher listed volume 29 for November 6, 2026. That publication date is separate from the anime schedule and gives no indication of which chapters the new television series will cover. For the shortest path to April, use the TV order above, add the specials if you want them, and check the official series site again once the premiere day is posted.

How much time do you need to catch up?

Think of the catch-up as four short TV runs rather than one uninterrupted season. If you watch two episodes a night, the original 13-episode run takes seven evenings, with one episode left for the last night. You can use that first season to decide whether to keep going. A returning viewer who mainly wants to get reacquainted with the voices can instead start with the teaser and revisit a few episodes of Tadaima.

The title changes also give you useful stopping points. Finish one TV season before switching subtitles, especially between Okawari and Okaeri, whose names are easy to mix up when results are sorted alphabetically. Save a note with the year and title if you are moving between catalogs. If you come across a separate listing for Natsuyasumi, keep it with the extras after Tadaima and leave the fifth-season slot for Korekara.
