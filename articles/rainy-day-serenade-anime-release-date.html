Rainy Day Serenade Anime Release Date and English Manga Guide

The Rainy Day Serenade anime premieres in Japan in April 2027. The first English manga omnibus arrives January 19, 2027, collecting Japanese volumes 1 and 2. The anime’s exact premiere day and international streaming service remain unannounced. Here is how to start reading and what the first official teaser confirms.

When does Rainy Day Serenade come out?

Rainy Day Serenade begins broadcasting in Japan in April 2027. The November 6 announcement establishes the month and introduces the first teaser and key visual. It leaves the exact premiere day open. If you are setting a reminder, use April as the window to check for an update from the anime’s production team. There is no confirmed weekly episode time to convert yet.

January 19, 2027 is the listed publication date of the first English manga omnibus. That date belongs to Kodansha Comics’ paperback and comes from the distributor’s book listing. The two dates are useful together because they give readers a chance to start the story in English before the television broadcast begins. A bookseller’s preorder page tells you when to expect the book. The anime website is the source for the television schedule.

You may encounter the title written as Namidaame to Serenade, or as 涙雨とセレナーデ on Japanese announcements. Use Rainy Day Serenade to search English bookstores and the Japanese title to locate the production’s posts. The author’s name, Haruka Kawachi, helps distinguish this work from similarly titled manga and anime when searching either catalog.

Where to start the English manga

Start with Rainy Day Serenade Omnibus 1. Kodansha Comics’ first English paperback collects Japanese volumes 1 and 2. The distributor lists 336 pages and the ISBN 9798888779255. If a store describes it as “Vol. 1–2,” that refers to the contents of a single book. You can use the ISBN to check whether a listing for “Volume 1” is actually the English omnibus.

The number on the spine matters when comparing editions. The first English omnibus covers the opening two volumes, while a Japanese volume numbered 1 is only the first of those two. If you decide to continue in Japanese after finishing the English paperback, the next original volume to look for is Volume 3. Readers who want to continue in English can check the distributor for the next omnibus and its contents once listed. The January date and ISBN apply specifically to the first paperback.

The manga ran in Kodansha’s Kiss magazine and the original series has 14 volumes. That is a count of the Japanese books. It does not tell you how quickly the English edition will be published or how much of the manga the anime will cover. If you prefer to read before watching, the first omnibus is a clear entry point. If you prefer to discover the story on screen, you can start with Episode 1 when the anime premieres and decide later whether to read from the beginning.

Where will the anime stream?

The April announcement applies to Japanese broadcasting. The official materials reviewed for this guide do not identify an international streaming service, a simulcast schedule or an English dub. If you are in the United States, Canada or another market outside Japan, check for a distributor announcement that names both the series and your territory. A Japanese broadcast month alone cannot tell you whether episodes will be available abroad on the same day.

The English manga license and anime distribution are separate. Kodansha Comics can publish the book in English while the television series still awaits an overseas streaming announcement. When checking a platform’s listing, distinguish spoken audio from subtitles. An English title in the interface can appear on a Japanese-audio release, and a subtitled premiere date can differ from a later dub date. AniNextUp’s release calendar tracks the date once announced, while the streaming guide organizes confirmed platforms.

What is Rainy Day Serenade about?

Hina Katagiri is a present-day high school student with a recurring dream of meeting a boy in a garden. One day she finds herself in that setting and discovers she has traveled back to 1907. Takaaki Hongo takes her for Hinako, the woman he is engaged to. The confusion becomes more complicated when Hina meets Hinako and sees how closely they resemble each other.

The premise gives the story two mysteries that work together. Hina wants to understand why a place from her dreams exists in another era. At the same time, people around her read her face as proof that she belongs in a family and a relationship she barely knows. For a spoiler-light start, pay attention to which name other characters use when they address her. That small detail helps separate the roles of the two young women even as the story uses their resemblance to complicate each encounter.

The year 1907 puts the period setting in Japan’s Meiji era. This is useful context for the teaser’s contrast between a modern student and the streets and dress of the past. It also helps explain why the mistaken identity has consequences beyond an awkward introduction. Hina has entered a social world where other people already have expectations about Hinako, and those expectations shape what they think Hina should do.

Official teaser, cast and production

The November teaser is the first footage released by the production and introduces the voices of Hina and Takaaki. Yume Miyamoto voices Hina Katagiri, and Takeo Otsuka voices Takaaki Hongo. Tezuka Productions handles animation, with Takashi Kamei directing, Yoko Yonaiyama overseeing series composition and Nobuteru Yuki designing the characters. Use the teaser to get a sense of the atmosphere and hear the Japanese leads. The first preview does not establish an episode count or the manga chapters the season will adapt.

The official key visual places Hina and Takaaki in a period street and uses the reflection beneath them to suggest the garden and the childhood figures from the story. That is a useful image to revisit after the teaser: it shows the two time frames side by side without revealing how they connect. Readers deciding between the manga and the anime can sample the teaser first, then use the paperback listing to see which opening volumes are included in English.

What to check before April

The production also announced an original-art exhibition to mark the anime adaptation and the manga spin-off. That announcement belongs to the franchise’s publication and event calendar. If you encounter an exhibition listing while searching for the anime, check whether the date refers to the event, the paperback or the broadcast. The official site keeps those announcements separate, and the first teaser notice is the one to follow for details about the adaptation itself.

For the manga, use the distributor’s listing to confirm the edition, date and ISBN before ordering. Retail availability can differ between stores, especially for readers importing an English paperback. For the anime, watch for three separate details: the exact broadcast date in Japan, the platform carrying the series in your country, and the languages offered there. These details can arrive in different announcements. A confirmed Japanese premiere can go on your calendar while the streaming plan for your region is still pending.

If you plan to read one book before the anime begins, the January omnibus gives you the story’s opening in order. You can stop after that volume and return to the adaptation without trying to predict where the first season will end. The first promotional video is enough to preview the central mystery. Save later character descriptions for after you have started the story if you prefer to avoid details about how Hina’s relationships develop.
