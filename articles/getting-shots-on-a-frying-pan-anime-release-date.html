Getting Shots on a Frying Pan Anime Release Date, Trailer & Manga Guide

When does Getting Shots on a Frying Pan come out?

Getting Shots on a Frying Pan is scheduled to premiere in Japan in January 2027. Asmik Ace announced the window on November 12, 2026, alongside a teaser and the two voice actors playing Juntaro. The announced broadcasters are Nippon TV, Yomiuri TV, BS Nippon TV and AT-X. The first episode’s day and airtime have yet to be announced. Use January as the release window when adding the series to your watchlist.

The title you will see on the anime’s Japanese site is 鍋に弾丸を受けながら. The production calls it Nabetama for short, and its logo uses Getting Shots on a Frying Pan. Romanized titles vary, so the nickname or Japanese spelling can be a better way to find the same show in broadcaster listings. The production’s November update establishes the same window for the four named networks without giving separate time slots for them.

Where can you watch Nabetama?

Japanese television is the confirmed release route. The November announcement gives no international streaming service, English subtitle rollout, dub or list of countries. If you watch outside Japan, look for an announcement that names a video service and specifies where it will carry the series. If you watch in Japan, check the schedule of the station you intend to use once a premiere day is published. A station’s announcement and a streaming catalogue answer different questions.

The teaser is available through the official anime site and the linked YouTube upload. That is the place to look if you want footage today. For the series itself, check for an episode listing under the exact title before assuming that a platform has picked it up. A trailer appearing on an official YouTube channel gives viewers a preview, while any full-episode release requires its own announcement. The AniNextUp release calendar is another place to check for dated anime premieres as the winter season approaches.

Watch the official Nabetama teaser

Asmik Ace’s teaser accompanies the January broadcast announcement. It opens with Juntaro explaining his approach to finding exceptional food, then shows how the series combines restaurant visits and travel with his distinctive way of seeing people. If you want to understand the visual premise before opening the manga, start here. The teaser is also the quickest way to match the English logo to the Japanese title you will see on official schedules.

Why do two actors play Juntaro?

Shu Uchida voices Juntaro as he appears through his own filter, and Masaaki Mizunaka plays Juntaro as he appears in reality. They are credited for two presentations of the same protagonist. In the story, Juntaro’s long immersion in illustrated media leads him to picture every person he encounters, including himself, as a beautiful girl. The anime uses the two performances to express the difference between that viewpoint and the real-world man behind it.

That distinction helps make sense of the promotional art. If you see a girl in a sun hat in the footage and then read a synopsis about a man on a food trip, both are describing Juntaro. It also explains why the casting announcement gives both actors the lead. The split is part of the adaptation’s storytelling, and the credits indicate which portrayal each actor voices. English dub casting, if a dub is announced, would be a separate matter.

J.C.STAFF is producing the animation, with Masahiro Hiraoka directing. Asmik Ace credits Susumu Yamakawa for series composition and scripts, Natsuki Oyama for character designs and chief animation direction, Yoshihide Otomo for music, and Miwa Iwanami for sound direction. The official credits name Juntaro Aoki and Shin Moriyama as the creators of the manga. Aoki is the writer and Moriyama is the artist.

What is Getting Shots on a Frying Pan about?

Juntaro travels to try dishes he is unlikely to encounter in a conventional guidebook. The publisher presents the source as nonfiction food reporting with a deliberately exaggerated visual point of view. Each encounter can start with a local specialty, a fishing trip or a conversation that introduces him to a meal. Food is the reason to go somewhere, and the people he meets explain why the meal matters there.

There are two things to follow in the premise. The first is the food reporting: what a dish is, who makes it and why it is worth a detour. The second is how Juntaro remembers and presents the encounter. He might describe a real place or ingredient, while the animation makes the people around him look like characters from another story. The unusual rendering is part of the account from the beginning, and the two Juntaro voices give the anime another way to mark that distinction.

The official teaser leads with Juntaro’s enthusiasm for unexpected food and shows the pace the adaptation is aiming for. A viewer looking for a quiet cooking series may be surprised by the emphasis on travel, risk and surprise. A viewer who enjoys food stories built around a new place and the people who live there has a clearer reason to try the first episode. The preview shows the premise in motion without establishing how many destinations or chapters the first season will cover.

Where can you read the Nabetama manga?

Start with the original series on KADOKAWA’s KadoComi manga page. The publisher lists the title in Japanese and offers an entry for the first chapter. Look for the credits to Juntaro Aoki and Shin Moriyama when checking a bookstore or digital listing. An official English edition could not be verified from the publisher listings checked for this guide.

KADOKAWA also lists Nabetama Transit, a separate manga spinoff drawn by Hirota Takahara. If you want the source credited in the anime announcement, start with the original series listing without Transit in the title. Both listings use similar Japanese wording, so the subtitle and artist credit make a quick check before you buy or open a chapter.

If you want to compare the manga and the anime, begin with the first chapter and the official teaser. The opening manga listing identifies the story’s starting point, while the trailer lets you hear how the anime handles its voice performances. The production has yet to publish a chapter-by-episode breakdown, so a panel or dish shown in promotional material does not give viewers a reliable episode number. Readers who prefer to go in fresh can use the teaser alone to decide whether to watch.

Which release details are still pending?

The next useful update for Japanese viewers would be the exact premiere day and local airtime. International viewers can look for a territory-specific service announcement and check whether it lists subtitles or dubbed audio. For the manga, look for a publisher listing that explicitly names the language and format you want. The Japanese chapter page gives you a legitimate starting point today, and the November teaser gives you an early look at the TV adaptation. Both can be found under Nabetama even when a search uses a different romanization of the full title.

For the clearest comparison of the two performances, check the official cast notice alongside the teaser. The notice labels each portrayal and includes comments from both actors, so you can match the names to the versions of Juntaro without relying on a fan translation of the credits.
