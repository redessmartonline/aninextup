Skip and Loafer Season 2: April 2027 Release Window, Trailer & Where to Watch

Skip and Loafer Season 2 premieres in April 2027 and will stream on Crunchyroll. See the new trailer, meet two new cast members and catch up on Season 1.

When does Skip and Loafer Season 2 come out?

Skip and Loafer Season 2 begins airing in Japan in April 2027. The production announced that window in August and kept it in the November 16 update. The new announcement adds a trailer, the first key visual and two cast additions. As of November 16, the official site gives a month for the Japanese broadcast. The exact day, first episode time and weekly schedule are still open.

That distinction matters if you keep a seasonal calendar. April is firm enough to put the show on a spring watchlist, but it is too early to block out a specific evening. The series also appears under Skip to Loafer in some databases. Before you save two listings for the same sequel, check that the entry is marked as the second season and points back to the same production site.

Where to watch Skip and Loafer

Crunchyroll has announced that it will stream Season 2. Its August coverage confirms the platform alongside the April 2027 window. The announcement gives viewers a service to check once the sequel arrives. For a local watch plan, use the episode listing in your country when it becomes available. The Japanese broadcast time and the time an episode appears in your local catalog can be different.

If you are catching up now, find Skip and Loafer on Crunchyroll and begin with the first episode of the original series. The first season runs for 12 episodes, so a three-week rewatch at four episodes a week is enough to finish it. A slower approach works just as well for this particular show. Several of its best moments are small reversals in a conversation, the sort that land harder when you still remember how those two people first met.

For subtitle and audio options, check the episode player in your region. A language option on an existing season does not guarantee the same options for a new one. The November announcement names the Japanese cast. Treat any English dub date, weekly upload time or episode count circulating without a platform listing as provisional.

What did the November trailer announcement reveal?

The production released its first full promotional video for Season 2 alongside a key visual and character information on November 16. That gives the sequel a more specific introduction than the August teaser. If you have seen conflicting upload dates, look for the trailer attached to the November news entry. The August teaser is still listed farther down the official page, with the earlier broadcast announcement.

Open the official Season 2 trailer on YouTube.

The cast announcement gives the trailer some useful context. Chieri Yasaka and Kiyohiko Ujiie are new names to learn, while Mitsumi Iwakura and Sosuke Shima still anchor the returning ensemble. The production included comments from the new actors about how their characters handle other people. Those comments offer a way into the new cast without needing to read ahead in the manga.

If you are sharing the trailer with a friend who has never seen the series, the first episode is a better introduction to Mitsumi. The trailer assumes that her move to Tokyo and the people she met there already mean something to you. It can help a returning viewer recognize the familiar cast, but a newcomer will get more out of seeing how an apparently ordinary first day of school brings Mitsumi and Shima together.

Who are the new Season 2 cast members?

Ueda plays Chieri Yasaka. Her comment on the official site focuses on the distance between the smile Chieri shows other people and what that expression might conceal. Takenaka plays Kiyohiko Ujiie. His comment describes a person whose insecurities and pride affect how he relates to Mitsumi and her friends. Those are descriptions from the performers, so they are best read as clues to the characters rather than a synopsis of their storylines.

The returning cast also includes Yuka Terasaki as Mika Egashira, Maaya Uchida as Yuzuki Murashige and Megumi Han as Makoto Kurume. Together, those three make a good reason to rewatch beyond Mitsumi and Shima’s scenes. Mika pays close attention to how people present themselves, and the friendship between Yuzuki and Makoto makes first impressions look particularly unreliable. Those threads are worth remembering when the sequel adds two people who have their own ideas about how they want to be seen.

P.A. Works is back for animation production. Kotomi Deai returns as director and is credited for series composition alongside Yoko Yonaiyama. The production also lists Manami Umeshita for character design and chief animation direction. These continuity details matter for a series that often lets a hesitant look or a pause do more than a long exchange of dialogue.

What should you remember from Season 1?

Mitsumi moves from rural Ishikawa to Tokyo with her school years mapped out in her head. She is capable and ambitious, but that preparation cannot teach her the unwritten rules of a room she has just entered. Shima helps her on her first day. The friendship that follows gives each of them a different way to navigate school, particularly when being polite and saying what they actually think pull in different directions.

On a rewatch, pay attention to what the characters assume about each other when they are alone compared with what they say in a group. Mitsumi often takes people at their word, which gives them room to reconsider the front they have been putting up. Sometimes it also means she misses what they were trying to imply. That double edge is part of the series’ humor. It also explains why a story with ordinary school events can make an offhand remark feel like a turning point.

The school festival toward the end of the first season is especially worth revisiting before the sequel. It brings different social circles into the same space and gives Shima more to do than guide Mitsumi around school. It also lets the supporting cast react to each other without a character having to explain everyone’s history. If you have time for only the final stretch before April, start early enough to see the preparations as well as the festival itself.

Should you read the manga while you wait?

Misaki Takamatsu’s original manga is available in English from Seven Seas Entertainment. Volume 1 is the easiest place to start if you want to read without making assumptions about where a particular episode leaves off. The opening covers Mitsumi’s arrival and gives you a chance to see how the timing of an awkward moment changes between a page turn and the animated version.

If you would rather experience Chieri and Kiyohiko’s roles through the anime, stop at the Season 1 rewatch and save the manga for later. Publisher descriptions for later volumes naturally discuss where relationships have gone. For the anime, the practical things to watch next are a specific April broadcast date, an episode listing for your country and the language options that listing actually carries.

The anime and the manga can also serve different moods. The anime lets you hear the gap between what a character means to say and how the line actually comes out. The manga gives you time to sit with a reaction before moving on. If you only want a taste of the source, try the opening chapter first. It has enough of the central relationship to help you decide whether to read ahead or keep the next season a surprise.
