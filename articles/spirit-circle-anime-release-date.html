What is Spirit Circle about? A spoiler-free introduction

Satoshi Mizukami’s manga follows Fuuta Okeya, a fourteen-year-old who sees ghosts. A bandage conceals a mark on his cheek. When transfer student Kouko Ishigami recognizes it, she treats him as an enemy connected to earlier lives. What initially resembles an awkward school encounter becomes a mystery about identities, memory and responsibility.

The title also refers to an object within the story. Seven Seas’ second-volume description explains that spirit circles let Fuuta revisit past lives, with a spirit called Rune involved in his journey. That makes reincarnation more than background lore: it gives the characters a way to investigate why their present relationship is so hostile.

Our reading of the premise is that its strongest hook is a moral problem. If someone accuses you of wrongdoing from a life you cannot remember, what would count as an answer? Learning the facts and accepting another person’s judgment are different things. That tension offers a reason to follow the mystery beyond simply waiting for a stronger supernatural ability.

Seven Seas classifies the series across fantasy, period-piece, science-fiction and supernatural categories. Readers should therefore expect the premise to reach beyond a single contemporary school setting. This is context from the published manga descriptions, not a review of the unreleased television episodes.

Will 18 episodes adapt the complete manga?

The episode total tells us the announced television length; it does not provide a chapter-by-chapter adaptation plan. The English manga runs through six volumes, but dividing those books equally across eighteen episodes would be a prediction, not an official schedule.

That distinction matters if you are deciding where to start reading after watching. Adaptations can combine scenes, move information earlier or spend different amounts of time on individual chapters. Until episodes establish a reliable comparison, volume one is the straightforward starting point. It preserves the sequence in which the manga introduces its clues.

For the same reason, an eighteen-episode count cannot establish an ending date. We would need the premiere day and release pattern, including any scheduled breaks. Nor does that number by itself confirm a second season. Readers looking for a complete story already have the manga as an option; viewers awaiting the adaptation should keep its coverage and its weekly timetable as separate questions.

Japanese cast, staff and animation studios

  • Kazuki Ura: Fuuta Okeya
  • Anna Nagase: Kouko Ishigami
  • Eri Yukimura: Rune
  • Yuichi Nakamura: East

Anna Nagase also performs the ending theme, Koko ga Donna Sekai demo. NAGOMI and EOTA handle animation, with Twin Engine producing. Tatsuo Sato is credited for direction and series composition, and Satoru Fujimoto for character design. Twin Engine identifies the project as Sato’s final directorial work, completed by the team with assistant director Kentaro Mizuno after Sato’s death in April 2026.

For readers unfamiliar with those company names, Twin Engine’s studio overview describes EOTA as an organization supporting collaboration across its production group, including shared production resources. That helps explain why a project can carry both NAGOMI and EOTA credits. A production credit describes responsibility; it is not, by itself, a verdict on the finished animation. We will reserve that assessment for released material.

How to read Spirit Circle in English before the anime

Seven Seas lists the English manga in six volumes. Read them in numerical order, from volume one through volume six. Its final-volume listing explicitly identifies the conclusion, so this is a completed reading option rather than a series whose ending readers must wait for.

The first English volume is credited to translator Jocelyne Allen, adaptation writer Ysabet Reinhardt MacFarlane and letterer Lys Blakeslee. Its ISBN is 978-1-626926-01-1, which is useful when checking a bookstore or library catalog. Search by that identifier if a title search mixes editions or languages.

If you want the anime to reveal the mystery first, stop at the first-volume introduction and avoid later-volume descriptions. Publisher summaries for later books can explain revelations the opening deliberately withholds. You do not need to investigate those revelations to understand the announcement or decide whether the premise interests you.

If you prefer reading ahead, begin at the start and follow the volume numbers rather than an unofficial chronological order of past lives. The difference is between the order events happen within the fictional history and the order a reader learns about them. Preserving the latter keeps the investigation intact.
