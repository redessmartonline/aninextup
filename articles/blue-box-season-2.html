When did Blue Box Season 2 start?

Blue Box Season 2 premiered on October 4, 2026, with new episodes weekly on Netflix. Netflix's September 15 lineup announcement names the premiere and release pattern. The Japanese anime website independently confirms an October 4 television start on the TBS network.

For viewers catching up now, that means the season is in a weekly rollout rather than a complete-season drop. The premiere date marks the beginning of the schedule; it does not mean every episode became available that day. Check the episode list in your own Netflix catalog before planning a full-season marathon.

What time does Blue Box air in Japan?

The official Japanese schedule lists Sundays at 4:30 p.m. Japan Standard Time on the TBS network's 28 stations. JST is UTC+09:00, so that broadcast slot corresponds to 7:30 a.m. UTC. This is a Japanese television time, not a verified Netflix release time for other countries.

Netflix's announcement does not provide a territory-by-territory clock time. Converting the TBS broadcast into your local timezone therefore does not establish when a new Netflix episode will unlock. The regional Netflix episode listing remains the appropriate place to check streaming availability.

Where can you watch the new season?

Netflix confirms the weekly Season 2 release. Its official trailer also directs viewers to the service. The Japanese website separately lists domestic streaming services and warns that starting dates can vary by platform. Those Japanese distribution listings should not be treated as international licensing announcements.

If Season 2 is absent from your catalog, check the season selector on the existing Blue Box title page and confirm your regional listing. This guide does not promise identical availability, subtitle choices or dubbing options in every country. Those details can change independently of the Japanese broadcast schedule.

What is the story, without major spoilers?

Blue Box follows Taiki Inomata, a badminton player at Eimei High, and Chinatsu Kano, the older student whose dedication to basketball catches his attention. Their relationship changes when Chinatsu starts living with Taiki's family. School, training and the awkwardness of sharing everyday space give the romance its setting.

The useful distinction for newcomers is that the sports are part of the characters' lives, not simply a backdrop for dating. Taiki's ambition and Chinatsu's commitment create reasons for early mornings, missed opportunities and difficult conversations. Watching how those commitments affect their choices is a stronger starting point than expecting the season to focus only on confessions.

Who is Yumeka, and why does she matter?

Netflix highlights Yumeka as the new character complicating Taiki and Chinatsu's story. The official character profile identifies Yumeka Kido as Chinatsu's former mini-basketball teammate. She is a gifted player whom Chinatsu admired; the two grew apart after Yumeka moved because of family circumstances.

That history gives her arrival a connection to Chinatsu's sporting identity, beyond introducing another face into the school cast. It is worth remembering that connection while watching, but the promotional premise does not settle how the characters' disagreements will end. This guide avoids importing later manga outcomes into the anime's release information.

Should you finish Season 1 first?

Yes. Season 2 continues an established relationship and school-sports story. Start with Season 1 to understand the household arrangement and the friendships around Taiki, Chinatsu and Hina. A trailer can remind a returning viewer of the atmosphere, but it cannot replace the gradual changes that make the sequel's interactions meaningful.

The original manga is by Kouji Miura. VIZ publishes the English edition and offers an official series page for readers who want to explore the source material. We are not assigning a manga chapter as the anime's continuation point: the sources reviewed here do not establish an exact Season 2 adaptation endpoint.

Who makes Season 2?

Netflix names Daisuke Sakou as director and Electric Circus as the animation studio, matching the Season 2 credits on the Japanese website. Shoya Chiba returns as Taiki and Reina Ueda as Chinatsu. The official Japanese cast listing also credits Yoshino Aoyama as Yumeka.

The official music announcement names aiko's “Anata no Hana no Iro” as the opening and Elsewhere Kikou's “blue in” as the ending. These credits help distinguish the sequel's production information from Season 1 material that may still appear in older search results.

Watch the official Netflix Anime trailer

Netflix Anime published the official trailer on August 31, 2026 at 5:00:01 p.m. UTC−07:00, equivalent to September 1 at 00:00:01 UTC. This exact timestamp comes from the original YouTube upload metadata. The trailer runs two minutes and fifteen seconds.

Episode count and finale: what is still unknown?

The Netflix lineup announcement confirms weekly episodes but does not give a complete Season 2 episode count or a finale date. We will not calculate an ending date from an assumed number of episodes. Broadcast changes and regional streaming updates must be checked against official notices as the season progresses.
