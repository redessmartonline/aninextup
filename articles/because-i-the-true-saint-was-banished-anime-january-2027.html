What is the story about?

Eliane supports her kingdom as a saint, but Prince Claude breaks off their engagement and expels her. She decides to leave that relationship behind. After helping Nigel, a prince from a neighboring country, she is invited to stay at his castle and starts putting her abilities to use in a different setting.

The official introduction frames the story around Eliane's fresh start. Her healing power matters, but the premise also centers on rebuilding her life after rejection.

Confirmed Japanese cast and animation studio

Masumi Tazawa voices Eliane, while Taku Yashiro plays Nigel. The production's character profiles describe Eliane as a capable, determined saint and Nigel as a considerate prince.

Tsumugi Akita Anime Lab is producing the animation. Tsukasa Sakurai directs, Naoki Tozuka handles series composition and Naru Nishikori designs the characters. The music credit is Harakanako. These credits are listed on the official staff and cast section; they do not specify an episode total.

Which original work is being adapted?

The source novels are written by Shikiso Utsuzawa with original character designs by Pukyunosuke. Mokuba Matsu draws the manga adaptation. The official books section distinguishes the novel and comic editions. Readers may also encounter the Japanese title Shin no Seijo de Aru Watashi wa Tsuihou Saremashita. Why does Eliane leave her former kingdom?

The official introduction adds context beyond the short “banished saint” premise. Eliane had served Belkaim as its true saint, but Prince Claude suddenly ends their engagement and expels her. The same introduction says she is tired of his mistreatment and infidelity, so leaving is also her decision to begin a different life. She later helps Nigel, the neighboring kingdom’s prince, after he is injured in a monster fight. His invitation to stay at his castle gives her a place to use her abilities without returning to the relationship she left. That setup makes the title’s promise of a happy restart more than a simple story of a royal punishment.

No prerequisite reading is named in the anime announcements. That is useful context if you want to compare versions, but it does not confirm that the anime will adapt a specific volume or follow every manga scene. New viewers can begin with the television series when it airs; readers interested in the source can choose the novels or manga according to the format they prefer.
