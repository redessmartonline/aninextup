When does A Pen, Handcuffs, and a Common-Law Marriage come out?

The Japanese television premiere is January 9, 2027 at 25:00 JST on TOKYO MX and BS11. In ordinary calendar notation, that means January 10 at 1:00 a.m. in Japan. The production announced the exact slot on November 20, alongside the main visual. Both dates describe the same broadcast, rather than separate premieres.

Japanese late-night television schedules often keep an after-midnight show under the previous evening’s programming date. To enter this one in a calendar app, use January 10 at 01:00 with the Asia/Tokyo time zone. Entering January 9 at 01:00 would put your reminder a full day early. The stated weekly slot is Saturday at 25:00, which becomes early Sunday when written on a conventional calendar.

Where can you watch it? Is Crunchyroll or Netflix confirmed?

No streaming service is named in the official broadcast section checked on November 21, 2026. The production website lists TOKYO MX and BS11 for television, while the streaming field still says “Coming Soon…”. That does not confirm Crunchyroll, Netflix or another international platform.

For now, save the title to your watchlist without buying a subscription solely for this series. When a streaming announcement arrives, check the countries covered, subtitle languages and whether the service specifies a weekly release. A Japanese television date alone cannot answer those questions. A platform carrying another EMT Squared production also does not establish that it holds rights to this one.

There is likewise no English-dub launch or complete episode-release calendar established by the announcements reviewed for this guide. An international premiere could match the Japanese broadcast, follow it or vary by country; the current information does not decide between those possibilities. What is the anime about?

The English manga publisher introduces Eiji Kirisame, a forty-year-old detective, and Tsugumi Kuchinashi, a silent high-school student encountered at a crime scene. Tsugumi communicates through drawings on a sketchbook. Her observations can help the investigation, but the pair’s unexpected connection also brings romantic-comedy complications.

That setup makes communication part of the mystery. A drawing needs interpretation rather than simply being accepted as a spoken statement. For a prospective viewer, the useful question is whether that mix of visual clues and an awkward investigative partnership appeals to you. The promotional premise does not establish how demanding every case will be or whether the adaptation will finish a particular manga storyline.

Who voices the leads, and which studio is making it?

The production credits Tomoaki Maeno as Eiji Kirisame and Inori Minase as Tsugumi Kuchinashi. The November 20 update adds Yusuke Kobayashi, Sumire Morohoshi and Sora Amamiya to the cast, introducing more of the people around the central partnership.

EMT Squared produces the animation. The official staff listing names Takaaki Ishiyama as director, Shigeru Murakoshi for series composition and Miyuki Nakamura for character design. These are the announced production credits; they do not by themselves indicate an episode count or how many volumes the season will adapt.

What are the opening and ending songs?

Inori Minase performs the opening, Mikaiketsu de, Romance de., and saeco performs the ending, Message. Minase’s official discography separately lists the opening as her fourteenth single, with a January 27, 2027 CD release and writing, composition and arrangement by Aira.

The single’s date is later than the anime’s television launch. It is a music-product release, not a revised date for episode one. The PV offers an early sample of the songs; a song excerpt does not mean that a full episode is available. When saving dates, label the television premiere and the music release separately.

Can you read the manga in English before the anime?

Yes. Yen Press lists an English edition of Volume 1, credited to writer Shinichi Sawaragi and artist Tank Gasuyama. It is a way to try the original story before the television premiere, without needing to infer the premise from trailer clips alone.

Start with the first volume if you want the introduction to the partnership. Jumping to a later volume because it has a newer publication date risks skipping the setup. The book’s availability and the anime’s streaming rights are separate: an English manga edition does not confirm an English dub, subtitles or a viewing service.

How to find the correct English book listing

The publisher uses the full English title, including the words A Pen, Handcuffs, and a Common-Law Marriage. The first volume’s ISBN is 9798855417913, also shown in Hachette’s regional book listing. A bookstore’s publication or delivery date concerns that edition of the manga; it should not replace the television date at the top of this page. Reading the source first is optional, but it gives you a fuller introduction than promotional footage.
