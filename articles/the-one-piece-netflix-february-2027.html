THE ONE PIECE Release Date: February 2027, Episodes & Where to Watch

When does THE ONE PIECE come out on Netflix?

THE ONE PIECE is scheduled to premiere globally on Netflix in February 2027. Netflix has confirmed the month but has not announced a calendar day or release hour. If you are planning a watch date, treat February as the confirmed window and check the title’s local Netflix page closer to launch; do not convert the month into an assumed February 1 premiere.

The platform plan is clearer than the timing. Netflix says all seven episodes will be released together, so viewers can watch the announced first season in one visit rather than wait for a weekly episode. The official announcement does not give a regional time table, however. A Netflix release in one country should not be turned into an exact local time for every territory without a later platform listing.

How many episodes are in the first season?

Season 1 is planned for seven episodes and about 300 minutes in total. Dividing that approximate runtime by seven works out to roughly 43 minutes per episode, but that is only an average calculation. Netflix has not published the individual runtimes, so it does not confirm that every installment will be the same length or say how opening and ending credits affect each episode’s listed duration. The seven-episode count and all-at-once drop answer two different questions: how much of the new adaptation is planned, and how its launch is structured. Neither announcement confirms how many seasons the project will have. Netflix has also not described a later season schedule or said when it would decide whether to continue the adaptation.

Which part of the manga will THE ONE PIECE adapt?

The new series starts from the beginning of Eiichiro Oda’s manga and adapts the East Blue Saga. Netflix says its first season will cover the opening 50 chapters and lead up to Luffy’s encounter with Sanji at the floating restaurant Baratie. That gives readers a useful boundary for this season: it follows the early formation of Luffy’s pirate crew, rather than jumping directly into a later Grand Line storyline.

The East Blue material introduces the premise that shapes the larger adventure. Monkey D. Luffy sets out to become King of the Pirates and find the One Piece treasure, gathering companions as he crosses paths with other pirates and the people who stand in their way. Netflix describes the adaptation as a fresh yet familiar retelling of that beginning. It has not released an episode-by-episode chapter map, so the 50-chapter scope should not be used to guess exactly where a particular scene will appear. Is this a remake or a continuation of the original anime?

Netflix identifies THE ONE PIECE as a new adaptation that begins again with Chapter 1. It is separate from the long-running television anime, which began in 1999 and has continued for more than two decades. The announcement does not say that the existing series is ending, being replaced, or required viewing before the new version; the new project simply offers another starting point for the same manga story.

That distinction also helps separate this title from Netflix’s live-action ONE PIECE series. THE ONE PIECE is animated by WIT Studio and re-adapts Oda’s manga. It is not the next season of the live-action show, and the live-action release calendar does not establish the animated series’ episode dates.

Who is producing the new anime?

WIT Studio leads animation production, with Shueisha, Fuji Television Network and Toei Animation named as collaborators. Netflix’s published creative credits list Masashi Koizuka as director, Hideaki Abe as assistant director, and Taku Kishimoto for series composition. Kyoji Asano and Takatoshi Honda are credited for character design and chief animation direction. The listed team also includes creature designer and image-board artist Yasuhiro Kajino, prop designer Eri Taguchi, action animators Ken Imaizumi and Shûhei Fukuda, art director Tomonori Kuroda, and animation producer Ryoma Kawamura. Mayumi Tanaka is confirmed to return as the Japanese voice of Luffy in the new adaptation, according to the ONE PIECE franchise’s official announcement. A full Japanese cast list and the English-language dub cast have not been announced in the Netflix materials reviewed for this update. Viewers should therefore treat Tanaka’s role as a confirmed individual casting detail, not evidence that every original anime performer is returning.

Netflix’s verified YouTube channel lists the same June 24 publication date. The time above is the announced release time for the teaser, not the premiere time for the anime series. What is still unknown about the release?

The current confirmed plan is specific: February 2027, Netflix, seven episodes released together, about 300 minutes overall, and an East Blue adaptation ending at the Baratie meeting described by Netflix. The exact premiere day and hour, individual episode lengths, language options by region, and any future-season order remain unannounced in the official sources reviewed here.
