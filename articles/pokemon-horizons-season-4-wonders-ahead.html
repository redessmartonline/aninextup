Pokémon Horizons: Season 4—Wonders Ahead now has a firm U.S. streaming date. Part 1 premieres on Netflix on February 27, 2027, with Liko and Roy returning for the next chapter of the current Pokémon anime.

What Part 1 means for the Netflix schedule

The February 27 announcement covers the first batch of Season 4 episodes in the United States, not necessarily the entire season or a worldwide same-day rollout. The announcement covers Part 1; it does not give release dates for later parts. Viewers following Liko and Roy should therefore distinguish the new season's start from its later release batches.

When does Pokémon Horizons Season 4 release?

Part 1 of Pokémon Horizons: Season 4—Wonders Ahead premieres February 27, 2027 in the United States. The Pokémon Company International announced the date on October 6 alongside the season's official trailer.

Where can you watch Pokémon Horizons Season 4?

In the United States, Netflix is the confirmed exclusive streaming home for Part 1. The announcement specifically confirms the U.S. release, so viewers elsewhere should wait for territory-specific availability rather than assume the same date applies everywhere.

What changed in the October 6 announcement?

The update gave the English-language season its official Wonders Ahead subtitle, locked in the February 27 Netflix date for the U.S. and released a new official trailer. The announcement identifies the launch as Part 1; it does not establish dates for later parts.

What is Wonders Ahead about?

Season 4 continues with Liko and Roy after the Laquium threat. Their travels open into a new stretch of exploration and battles as they meet more Trainers and Pokémon. The new material also highlights Navie and Ditto among the encounters ahead.

Do you need to watch the earlier seasons first?

Wonders Ahead continues the existing Pokémon Horizons story rather than rebooting it. Netflix already carries the first three seasons in the U.S., making them the clearest catch-up path before Part 1 arrives.

How to avoid confusing Part 1 with the full season

A batch label is important: it identifies the beginning of the English-language Season 4 rollout, not the number of episodes in the full season. Fans outside the United States should look for an announcement from their regional Pokémon distributor instead of converting February 27 into a presumed worldwide launch.
