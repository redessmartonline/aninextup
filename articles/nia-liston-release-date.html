When did Nia Liston premiere?

Nia Liston: The Merciless Maiden began broadcasting in Japan on October 6, 2026. The official anime site lists it as a two-consecutive-cour television series, and the anime is now airing. Its current Japanese schedule is more useful than an old “coming this fall” listing: the premiere date has passed, and the series continues weekly.

The broadcaster schedule uses Japan’s late-night convention. TOKYO MX airs the show Tuesdays at 9:25 p.m. JST. BS Nippon TV lists Tuesdays at 24:30, which is Wednesday at 12:30 a.m. by the calendar clock; MBS lists Tuesdays at 27:30, or Wednesday at 3:30 a.m. JST. Those are the same overnight broadcast slots described using the Tuesday television day. The official On Air page is the source for the channel times.

Where can you watch Nia Liston?

For viewers outside Japan, HIDIVE’s official October schedule lists Nia Liston: The Merciless Maiden as a Tuesday simulcast beginning October 6. HIDIVE availability depends on territory, so the listing confirms a service, not universal access. Check the platform’s current catalogue in your country before subscribing or planning a watch party.

In Japan, the official site lists ABEMA and d Anime Store for the earliest post-broadcast stream, Tuesdays at 9:55 p.m. JST. U-NEXT and Anime Hodai follow on Fridays at 9:55 p.m. JST, with other services rolling out from October 12. These local service windows should not be confused with the HIDIVE simulcast or treated as evidence of availability in other countries.

How many episodes does season one have?

The official Blu-ray listing now provides a concrete episode count. BOX 1, scheduled for March 24, 2027, contains episodes 1–12; BOX 2, scheduled for June 23, contains episodes 13–25. Together with the official two-cour broadcast description, those listings support a 25-episode run. This is more precise than interpreting “two cours” on its own, which describes the broadcast span but does not always specify an episode total. What is the story about?

Nia is a noble girl whose fragile body once led to an early death. A powerful warrior’s soul is reborn in her, bringing a very different ambition: she wants to meet opponents strong enough to challenge her. The official synopsis frames the appeal around that contrast between an adorable young noblewoman and a battle-hungry former hero. The series is not only a succession of fights, however; Nia also attends school and participates in the kingdom’s “Magic Vision” entertainment business.

That second thread gives the premise a distinctive shape. Magic Vision is the fantasy world’s screen entertainment, and Nia’s involvement turns performance and public image into part of her daily life alongside combat. Official character notes connect Nia with Princess Hildetaura and rival talent Reliared in projects promoting the medium. This is context from the production’s published character descriptions, not a claim about future plot outcomes.

Who makes the anime?

KONAMI animation produces the series. Motoki Nakanishi directs, Gigaemon Ichikawa handles series composition, Yuki Ito and Kana Tosaoka are credited with character design, and Naoki Endo composes the music. Honoka Inoue voices Nia, Fuka Izumi plays Rinokis, Rina Honnizumi plays Hildetaura, and Rina Hidaka voices Reliared. The official staff and cast list has the full credits. The animation is adapted from Umikaze Minamino’s light novel, published under Hobby Japan’s HJ Bunko imprint. The official anime site also lists a manga adaptation by Kouta Koga. The official site currently lists twelve novel volumes and nine manga volumes, giving newcomers a clear way to distinguish the original prose series from its comic adaptation.

Where to find the source material

The official anime site lists Minamino’s original light novels separately from Kouta Koga’s manga adaptation: twelve novel volumes and nine manga volumes are currently listed. If you want the original version of Nia’s story, the novels are the source; the manga is a separate adaptation. The anime site does not publish a volume-by-volume map for the television episodes, so the Blu-ray episode ranges do not tell readers exactly where to resume in either book format.
