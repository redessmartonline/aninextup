Aoashi Season 2: Release Date and Where to Watch

What changes for Ashito in Season 2?

Season 2 begins after Aoi Ashito earns promotion to Tokyo City Esperion’s A team. That promotion changes the central challenge: he is now training alongside players with more experience and competing for a place in a stronger squad. The official first-cour visual brings together members of the youth A team, while the production describes Ashito as entering a new phase of competition. It sets up a clear question for viewers—how will he adjust his field awareness and decision-making when the standard around him rises?

This is a continuation of Ashito’s development, not a reset. His instincts remain important, but football at this level asks him to connect what he sees with the movement and responsibilities of his teammates. The season’s premise points toward that learning curve without confirming match outcomes or a specific endpoint for his progress.

When did Aoashi Season 2 premiere?

The season premiered in Japan on October 4, 2026. NHK E-Tele broadcasts it on Sundays at 5:00 p.m. JST. The official site lists Japanese subscription streaming on Hulu, Netflix, U-NEXT and Anime Times from 11:00 p.m. JST on Sundays. A wider group of services begins staggered Thursday releases from October 8 at 7:30 p.m. JST. These are domestic Japanese schedules, not universal times.

Where can international viewers watch?

Crunchyroll lists Season 2 for a set of international territories, including North America, Central and South America, Europe, Africa, Oceania, the Middle East, CIS, India and Southeast Asia. Its Sunday simulcast listing is 2:00 a.m. Pacific Time in supported regions. Availability can vary by territory and subscription, so confirm that the show and the newest episode appear in the catalog for your account. Japanese services such as Hulu Japan or U-NEXT should not be assumed to have the same access rules as services with similar names abroad.

The different Japanese release windows also explain why episode listings can look inconsistent. Some services carry the Sunday subscription stream, while other platforms begin on Thursday or offer transactional rentals. If an episode is not visible on a service, check its own schedule and region settings before treating the delay as a series-wide postponement.

What should new viewers know first?

The first season establishes Ashito’s move from a small local setting into Esperion’s youth system and introduces the coaches and teammates who shape his development. Season 2’s A-team promotion makes more sense after those earlier relationships and decisions. Watching Season 1 first is the clearest route for anyone who wants the competitive stakes and team dynamics to land as intended.

Returning viewers can use the promotion as the simple recap point: Ashito has reached a higher level, but that achievement is the start of another adjustment rather than proof that he has mastered the role. The series remains a sports drama about learning to read the game and work within a team, so the new squad creates both opportunity and pressure.

How many episodes are planned, and what does “cour” mean?

The official Aoashi site describes the first cour as the first 12 episodes out of 24 planned for Season 2. A cour is a broadcast block, so that announcement helps explain why the season has separate first- and second-cour schedules. It does not mean a streaming service must create a separate season page for each block. If you are catching up, compare the episode list and dates rather than relying only on a “new season” label. The official schedule and your service’s episode page are the best places to confirm the next release.

What time is the simulcast in the Dominican Republic?

Crunchyroll lists the international simulcast for Sunday at 2:00 a.m. Pacific Time. For Santo Domingo, that corresponds to 5:00 a.m. while Pacific Time is on daylight time. The United States ends daylight saving time on November 1, 2026; after the Pacific clock moves back to standard time, the same 2:00 a.m. Pacific listing corresponds to 6:00 a.m. in Santo Domingo. These are time-zone conversions, not a separate Dominican release announcement. Check the episode page in your local Crunchyroll catalog in case the service changes its schedule.
