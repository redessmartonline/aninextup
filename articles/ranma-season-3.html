Has Ranma 1/2 Season 3 started?

Yes. Season 3 began in Japan in the October 3, 2026 late-night television slot. Nippon TV lists Saturdays at 24:55 JST, which is Sunday at 00:55 on a standard calendar; the opening broadcast therefore fell on October 4 at 00:55 JST. The July production announcement confirmed that schedule together with exclusive Netflix distribution after the Japanese broadcast. A date listed as October 3 and a Japanese clock date of October 4 can both describe the same launch.

What does that mean for viewers in the US?

The first Japanese television start corresponds to October 3 at 15:55 UTC: 11:55 a.m. Eastern Daylight Time or 8:55 a.m. Pacific Daylight Time. Those are conversions of the television start, not independently announced Netflix upload times. The production says Netflix follows the broadcast, so it would be misleading to promise an episode at the converted start time. US daylight-saving changes later in the season also mean a fixed Japanese slot does not always convert to the same American clock hour.

Where can you watch the season?

Netflix’s own MAPPA anniversary announcement identifies Season 3 as coming worldwide and exclusively to Netflix. The anime’s Japanese production notice likewise says Netflix distribution follows the television broadcast. Use your local series page for the available episodes, subtitles and dubbed tracks. The official on-air site has separate season tabs, including older-season platform lists; services carrying an earlier season should not automatically be described as carrying Season 3 at launch. Is this a weekly release or a full-season drop?

The Japanese schedule is weekly, with the Netflix release linked to that broadcast. It is not an announcement that the full season appears at once. If you are planning a catch-up session, distinguish episodes already available in your catalog from later installments still awaiting their weekly release. Local broadcasters across the Nippon TV network also join on different schedules, so their listings may be behind the first Japanese broadcast without indicating a streaming delay.

What is Ranma 1/2 about?

Ranma Saotome and Akane Tendo are engaged through an arrangement made by their parents, but their martial-arts romantic comedy is anything but straightforward. During training in China, Ranma fell into the cursed springs of Jusenkyo. Cold water now transforms him into a girl, while hot water returns him to a boy. That complication feeds the misunderstandings, rivalries and slapstick around the couple. Season 3 continues this remake rather than introducing an unrelated protagonist or starting the story over.

What does the Season 3 trailer reveal?

Netflix’s June announcement highlights Ryoga Hibiki, whose rivalry with Ranma and feelings for Akane intensify in the new run. It also points to his newly mastered Breaking Point Blow. The July key visual surrounds Ranma, Akane and Ryoga with characters appearing in Season 3. Those are useful previews of the ensemble and the coming martial-arts complications, but neither a promotional image nor a trailer proves the season’s final manga chapter or the eventual resolution of the romance. Who are the returning Japanese cast members?

Kappei Yamaguchi voices Ranma, with Megumi Hayashibara voicing his female form, while Noriko Hidaka plays Akane. Koichi Yamadera returns as Ryoga, and Rei Sakuma as Shampoo. The production’s season-specific cast list also names Toshihiko Seki as Mousse and Kaori Nazuka as Ukyo Kuonji. These are Japanese cast credits. For a dubbed version, consult the credits and audio options of the episode you can actually access rather than assuming that every language track follows the same availability pattern.

Which studio and staff are making it?

MAPPA produces the animation, with Konosuke Uda directing, Kimiko Ueno handling series composition, Hiromi Taniguchi designing the characters and Kaoru Wada composing the music. Rumiko Takahashi’s manga is the original work, published by Shogakukan. The official staff page separates credits by season, so a list copied from the first season can miss later production changes. The roles above are supported by the Season 3 production information, rather than inferred from MAPPA’s involvement alone. Where should a new viewer begin?

Start with Seasons 1 and 2 of the current remake before Season 3. That is the clearest route through its recurring relationships, rivalries and running jokes. Watching an older Ranma adaptation can be enjoyable background, but it is not the same viewing order as continuing this MAPPA series. When searching Netflix, match the current adaptation’s season listing instead of using an episode number from a different version as your starting point.

Do not read the weekly television slot as a promise of a full-season drop or an announced manga ending.
