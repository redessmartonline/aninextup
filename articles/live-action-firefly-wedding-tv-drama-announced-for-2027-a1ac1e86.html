Live-Action Firefly Wedding TV Drama: 2027 Release Window, Cast and Story

Fuji TV confirms a 2027 live-action Firefly Wedding drama. See what is known about the broadcast, story, teaser, cast, manga and streaming status.

By AniNextUp Editorial Team

Firefly Wedding live-action drama: the confirmed 2027 release window

Fuji TV has confirmed that Firefly Wedding (Japanese title: Hotaru no Yomeiri) is being adapted into a live-action television drama for broadcast in 2027. The broadcaster identifies Fuji TV as the production copyright holder and names Oreco Tachibana’s manga as the source. It gives no month, premiere day, episode count, timeslot or production schedule. “2027” is the full release window currently confirmed; the anime’s schedule does not set the drama’s date.

The announcement came after the television anime’s first episode on October 9, 2026; Fuji TV’s news page was updated on October 10 in Japan. The anime and drama are separate adaptations. The anime’s premiere is not the drama premiere, and the formats can have different staff, casts and distributors.

What is the story about?

The manga is set during Japan’s Meiji era, a period of rapid social change. Satoko Kirigaya is a count’s daughter who hopes to make a marriage that benefits her family. After an assassin named Shinpei Goto targets her, she offers marriage as a way to survive. What begins as a desperate bargain becomes a dangerous relationship shaped by Shinpei’s intense attachment, Satoko’s changing feelings and the threats around them.

This describes the source material, not a scene-by-scene synopsis of the drama. Fuji TV has not published a drama-specific outline or confirmed how closely it will follow the manga. The original author welcomes seeing the characters in a real-world setting and says the live-action version may use different storytelling and expression. That is an invitation to expect its own interpretation, not a promise that every plot point will be reproduced unchanged.

Who is in the live-action cast?

No live-action actors have been named. Fuji TV says cast information and further news will be revealed later. Names associated with the anime belong to that separate production; they should not be presented as confirmed drama casting. The same applies to anime directors, character designers, composers and theme performers. Until Fuji TV or the drama’s official channels publish a cast list, the lead performers and creative staff remain unknown.

There is no confirmed episode total, filming start or drama-specific teaser beyond the short announcement PV. The clip establishes the adaptation and 2027 broadcast year; it is not a full trailer and does not reveal cast members. These are the key gaps readers should check for in future updates.

Is the drama connected to the 2026 anime?

They share a source manga but are independent screen adaptations. Fuji TV’s anime began in Japan on October 9, 2026, in the Noitamina programming block, with a listed Friday broadcast from 11:30 p.m. to midnight Japan time. That schedule applies only to the anime. The drama is planned for 2027, and Fuji TV has not said it will use the anime’s timeslot or production team.

This distinction answers common questions about whether the drama is “season two” or whether watching the anime is required first. The official notice describes a live-action adaptation of the manga, not an anime continuation. No viewing prerequisite has been announced. Viewers can follow the anime as its own version and later try the drama when its casting and release details arrive.

Where can viewers watch it?

Fuji TV is the confirmed Japanese broadcaster and production copyright holder. No overseas broadcaster, streaming service, subtitle plan or release outside Japan has been announced for the live-action drama. The anime has separate distribution information, but that does not establish that the drama will be licensed to the same service. International viewers should wait for a notice that explicitly names the live-action title and territory.

Shogakukan’s official e-comic listing identifies Oreco Tachibana as the manga’s author and presents the completed series as a 14-volume collection. This is a starting point for readers seeking the source story, though language editions and storefront availability vary by country. The anime’s official news archive tracks that adaptation separately; Fuji TV’s announcement is the primary source for drama developments.
