Wound & Bandage Anime Release Date, Japan Schedule & Streaming

Wound & Bandage premieres on January 9, 2027, at 11 p.m. JST on AT-X in Japan. ABEMA starts streaming Episode 1 at 2:30 a.m. JST on January 10, listed as Saturday 26:30. The November 6 main trailer previews MIYAVI’s opening theme. International streaming is still unconfirmed.

When does Wound & Bandage premiere?

The first screening on the regular Japanese television schedule is Saturday, January 9, 2027, at 11 p.m. JST on AT-X. The production lists ABC TV and the TV Asahi network in the ANiMAZiNG!!! programming block at Saturday 26:00, and ABEMA streaming at Saturday 26:30. The broadcaster and the streaming service both use Saturday as the label for a programming night that extends past midnight.

The easiest way to set a reminder is to convert the number before changing time zones. Subtract 24 from 26:30 and advance the calendar by one day. That gives you Sunday, January 10, at 2:30 a.m. in Japan. ABC TV’s 26:00 works the same way and becomes 2 a.m. JST on Sunday. AT-X uses 23:00, which stays on Saturday. All three listings can therefore belong to the same premiere announcement while showing different dates in a calendar app.

The earlier AT-X slot is a television broadcast. ABEMA is the first streaming option named in the Japanese announcement. If you see both described as coming first, check which kind of release is being discussed. On the published timetable, three and a half hours separate the AT-X slot from the ABEMA stream. The nationwide broadcast block sits between them, half an hour before ABEMA.

Some coverage of the November trailer prints January 9, 2026. The production’s broadcast announcement gives January 9, 2027, and the new trailer announcement uses the same 2027 date when describing the premiere-day special. Use those listings for reminders. The November date belongs to the promotional release, while January is when viewers can see the first episode.

Which ABEMA listing is Episode 1?

ABEMA has two distinct events on the premiere weekend. The production announced a preview special at 8 p.m. JST on January 9. Episode 1’s streaming slot is 2:30 a.m. JST on January 10. If you find a listing for 8 p.m., check its title for tokubetsu bangumi, the Japanese term used here for a special program. That event starts six and a half hours before the episode stream. The announcement says more details about the special will follow.

For readers comparing release times abroad, the scheduled ABEMA episode slot corresponds to Saturday, January 9, at 9:30 a.m. Pacific, 12:30 p.m. Eastern, 1:30 p.m. in Santo Domingo and 5:30 p.m. in the United Kingdom. Those clocks mark the same moment as 2:30 a.m. in Japan. Use them to interpret announcements or follow premiere-day discussion. Local streaming availability is a separate question that depends on who has the rights in your country.

Where can you watch outside Japan?

The Japanese production pages name AT-X, ABC TV and the TV Asahi network for television, and ABEMA for streaming in Japan. In the announcements reviewed for this guide, an international streaming partner, an English-subtitled simulcast and an English dub schedule are still unconfirmed. If you are watching from the United States, Canada or the United Kingdom, look for a distributor to list your territory and the title together before treating the Japanese times as your local release schedule.

A news story on a streaming company’s website can report an anime’s Japanese premiere without that company announcing a streaming license. The distinction matters here because Crunchyroll News covered the original teaser. A platform’s seasonal lineup or an episode page with regional availability would answer a different question. When you search in a catalog, try both Wound & Bandage and Kizuguchi to Hōtai. The Japanese title, 傷口と包帯, is useful on the production and broadcaster sites.

What is new in the November 6 trailer?

The latest promotion pairs the main trailer with a new key visual. The production says the trailer is the first preview of the main character voices, including Hisako Kanemoto as Riyo Washizu and Chikahiro Kobayashi as Kiritani. It also previews MIYAVI’s opening song, Show Me All Your Pain. That makes the new video the one to look for if you want to hear the actors and the theme together. Earlier character teasers remain useful if you want to see how the production introduced the cast in stages.

The announcement adds Ko Bonkobara and Kengo Takanashi as two men who work under Kiritani and names Tomokazu Sugita as narrator. Ginsuke Hatobane, voiced by Shoya Ishige, appears among the supporting characters. The main trailer gives those characters a place around Riyo and Kiritani, whose reactions drive the central joke. The presentation uses conspicuous panel-like compositions and held poses. Viewers deciding whether the visual style appeals to them can get a clearer impression from the trailer than from the poster alone.

What kind of story is Wound & Bandage?

The production site describes Ginsuke as a prospective match for Riyo who shares her interest in seeing Kiritani’s composure give way. That detail changes the shape of the setup. The character brought in as a potential romantic alternative has a reason to pay attention to the same person Riyo does. It also gives a first-time viewer a way to place Ginsuke in the trailer without mistaking him for one of Kiritani’s subordinates.

Kiritani, a senior figure in a yakuza group, is assigned to look after the boss’s daughter, Riyo. She is shy around other people, yet she becomes animated when someone who normally appears strong shows vulnerability. The assignment gives Kiritani an incentive to project confidence. It also puts him in close company with the person most likely to notice when that confidence cracks. The official introduction pitches the resulting mismatch as a romantic comedy.

Riyo has a name for her fascination, yowarā. The term appears in the Japanese promotion and is the focus of the ABEMA preview special. In the story, it is a character’s way of naming what she likes. It helps explain why the characters can read the same situation differently: a moment Kiritani would rather conceal is precisely the one Riyo finds worth paying attention to. That difference in perspective is a better guide to the tone than the title alone, which could suggest a drama about physical injuries.

The series adapts Umihoshi Nanai’s manga from Kodansha. Miyuki Ishida directs at J.C.STAFF, with Susumu Yamakawa credited for series composition and scripts and Natsuki Oyama for character design. If you are deciding whether to start with the manga, the adaptation begins with its own first episode in January. Readers who want to compare versions can look up the original on the production site’s manga information page. The premiere announcement provides the initial television and streaming slots. A complete episode count and finale date are still open.
