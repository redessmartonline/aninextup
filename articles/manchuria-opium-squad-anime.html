Manchuria Opium Squad Anime: Release Date, Story and Latest News

The 1937-set crime manga is getting an anime adaptation. Its first visual is out, but release and production details remain unannounced.

What is confirmed about the anime?

The anime adaptation of Manchuria Opium Squad was officially announced on October 5, 2026, alongside a first visual. Pony Canyon’s announcement identifies the project as an adaptation of Kodansha’s crime-suspense manga, but stops short of naming a release window or production team. This is an announcement of a project in development, not a premiere announcement: the format, studio, director, cast, broadcaster and streaming services are still unconfirmed in the official materials checked for this update.

The original story is by Tsukasa Monma. The manga artwork is now by Tsurushima, while Shikako is credited for the original art concept. The series returned to Kodansha’s Weekly Young Magazine in the same October issue that carried the anime news, so readers encounter two related developments at once: the comic is continuing with its new artist, and an animated adaptation is in the works. They are separate releases with separate schedules.

What is Manchuria Opium Squad about?

Manchuria Opium Squad is set in Manchuria in 1937. Its protagonist, Isamu Higata, is a soldier in the Kwantung Army who loses sight in his right eye during battle. He is reassigned to an agricultural unit, where he discovers poppies being grown as a source of opium. When his mother falls ill, his need to help her pushes him into illegal production. The official manga synopsis describes the choice as the turning point that sends both Isamu’s life and Manchuria’s fate in a new direction.

That setup gives the title its crime-suspense engine: a personal emergency becomes entangled with a dangerous trade and the people and powers that try to control it. The story’s appeal is not simply the historical backdrop or a lone criminal protagonist. It puts family duty beside the harm and danger attached to an illegal business, then follows the consequences of that decision. Those are story stakes, not a guide to producing or selling drugs; the official materials provide no technical how-to detail.

Historical setting and adaptation scope

The year and setting are confirmed by the publisher, but the manga is a work of fiction. Readers should not treat its characters, dialogue or individual events as a documentary account of real people. The official Japanese synopsis emphasizes the frontier setting and the way Isamu’s decision changes his fate and the region around him; it does not present a historical textbook or claim that his fictional case represents every person living in Manchuria at the time.

Is there an official trailer?

The announcement visual is a static image that assembles character drawings and production artwork around the series logo. It confirms an anime project, but it is not a trailer and does not reveal how the animation will look in motion. As of October 24, the official materials reviewed still do not provide a verified PV or trailer link. There is consequently no official video upload time to report, and no confirmed animation footage, theme song or episode count.

How popular is the manga?

What does the project’s popularity look like? Pony Canyon reported that the manga had exceeded 3.5 million copies in circulation by the announcement. That is a cumulative circulation figure supplied by the publisher, not a promise about anime audience size or a count of paid digital reads. It does explain why an adaptation announcement is a significant new milestone for a series that has already built a substantial readership.

Manga continuation and anime staff

For manga readers, the continuation is especially relevant. Young Magazine’s official listing says serialization has resumed and credits Monma for the original story, Tsurushima for the manga, and Shikako for the original art concept. The anime’s staff and format have not yet been confirmed. That distinction matters: the person drawing the continuing comic is named, but that alone does not tell readers which animation studio will adapt it or who will voice Isamu.

Is the manga suitable for younger readers?

The English-language digital listing is available from Kodansha USA. It labels the manga Mature 18+ and warns readers about graphic violence and drug abuse. That rating applies to Kodansha USA’s manga listing; it is not an announced age rating for the anime. The note is useful for readers choosing a series to start and for anyone deciding whether its subject matter is suitable for a younger or sensitive audience.

Release date and where to watch

When will the anime come out, and where can it be watched? Those are the two most practical questions, and neither has an official answer yet. No premiere month or year, Japanese broadcaster, international distributor, streaming platform or cast has been announced in the official notice. Avoid sites that attach a season or service to the title without linking a dated production announcement; a series page appearing in an anime database does not establish a release plan.

Where to follow official updates

For reliable updates, use the official anime site and official X account, and compare them with Pony Canyon’s announcement and Young Magazine’s series page. A dated broadcast notice, staff list, casting release, official video, or streaming partner announcement would be new information. Until one appears, the confirmed facts remain the adaptation, the announcement visual, the manga’s return under Tsurushima, and the story’s 1937 setting.

The existing manga is the way to read the story now; the anime is a separate adaptation whose format and release remain open. If you are new to the series, start with Kodansha’s official English listing for the premise and content advisory. If you already follow the manga, watch Young Magazine’s listing for its new chapter releases and the anime site for a future cast, staff or broadcast update.
