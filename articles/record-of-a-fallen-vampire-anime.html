When will The Record of a Fallen Vampire premiere?

The Record of a Fallen Vampire is scheduled to become a television anime in 2027. The October 5 announcement confirms the year, but not a season, month, date or weekly broadcast slot. A teaser and named production team give the project more substance than an adaptation announcement alone, yet neither establishes when episodes will arrive. As of October 24, a specific premiere date would go beyond the official information reviewed for this guide.

Where can you watch the anime?

No international streaming partner or territory list is named in the official teaser description. That means there is no confirmed Netflix, Crunchyroll or HIDIVE release to put on a viewing calendar yet. Pony Canyon publishes the promotional video, while ZERO-G and Liber handle animation production; those roles do not themselves identify the service that will distribute episodes in your country. Look for an explicit licensing or streaming announcement before treating a platform listing as settled.

What is the story about?

The central figure is Rose Red Strauss, the vampire king associated with the destruction of the Kingdom of Night a thousand years earlier. He is trying to recover his beloved queen, Adelheid, who has been sealed away. The official introduction places him in conflict with dhampirs hunting his life and the Black Swan, a powerful enemy. It also promises the gradual revelation of what happened a millennium ago, so the mystery of the past matters alongside the present-day battles.

Who is the Black Swan?

VIZ’s first-volume description explains the threat in more detail: the Black Swan inhabits a young human girl every 50 years and gives her the power to destroy the vampire king and queen. Each incarnation grows stronger. That premise creates a repeating danger across Strauss’s long search, rather than a single opponent he can simply outlive. Readers who want to avoid later manga revelations should stay with the first-volume synopsis; descriptions of subsequent books expose major changes in alliances and motivation.

Why do some listings call Strauss Akabara?

The Japanese anime credits identify the lead as Rose Red Strauss, voiced by Yuma Uchida. VIZ’s English manga listing uses Akabara in its synopsis. These are naming differences around the same central vampire king, not evidence of two protagonists or separate adaptations. For searches, the Japanese title Vampire Juujikai and the English title The Record of a Fallen Vampire lead to the same manga property by Kyo Shirodaira and Yuri Kimura.

Who are the confirmed voice actors?

Yuma Uchida plays Strauss, while Aino Shimada voices Bridget Irving Frostheart. The official teaser lists these two lead performers. Their presence should not be presented as a complete cast list: the queen and other characters mentioned in the premise do not gain confirmed voice actors merely because they are part of the story. Additional casting, dubbed-language performances and international audio options remain separate announcements to watch for.

Which studios and staff are making it?

Mankyu is responsible for directing and series composition. Animation production is credited jointly to ZERO-G and Liber, with character design by jimao and music by Evan Call. These are the roles listed in the original official video. The credits establish who is attached, but do not explain how the two studios divide individual episodes or production tasks. Call’s music credit also does not announce an opening singer, ending theme or soundtrack release date.

Should you read the manga first?

The source manga credits Kyo Shirodaira for the story and Yuri Kimura for the art. The official anime video identifies the original Square Enix edition as nine volumes. English readers can start with VIZ’s Volume 1 listing, which supplies the premise, creator credits and a Teen age rating. That label belongs to the manga edition, not an announced television classification. Starting at Volume 1 preserves the story’s gradual introduction of Strauss and the mystery surrounding his past.

Is the original manga complete?

Yes. VIZ’s series catalog identifies Volume 9 as the final volume, matching the nine-volume count in the official anime announcement. That gives prospective readers a bounded reading commitment rather than an unfinished monthly story to catch up with. You can read the original manga in volume order while waiting for the anime, although later book descriptions disclose twists that the first-volume premise keeps hidden. Completion of the source manga does not tell us how quickly the television adaptation will cover that material.

What does the anime announcement leave open?

The production has not stated an episode count or identified an adaptation endpoint in the reviewed materials. Nine manga volumes do not automatically imply nine episodes, a complete adaptation or multiple seasons. No broadcast hour exists to convert into a local viewing time yet. For the next meaningful update, follow the official anime site and social account, then check any future distribution notice for territories and language details. Those facts will determine when and how viewers can actually watch.
