The One. Later On… Anime: 2027 Release, Cast & Streaming Status

Here is the confirmed production information, the English manga starting point and what remains unannounced about release dates and streaming.

When does The One. Later On… anime come out?

The One. Later On… is confirmed as a television anime for 2027. Earth Star Entertainment announced the adaptation on November 22, 2026, and said further broadcast information would follow. The publisher’s announcement does not provide an exact day or month. A November 24 notice from the publisher and Pony Canyon’s current coverage reiterate the 2027 window.

Save the title under 2027 for now, rather than assigning it to winter, spring or another season. The November dates identify the announcement and follow-up reporting, not the start of the episodes. A year-only window also does not give viewers a weekly release day or establish that a complete season will arrive together.

Is Sono Mono. Nochi ni… the same series?

Yes. Sono Mono. Nochi ni… is the Japanese name associated with the original work and anime project. The One. Later On… is the English title used for Titan Manga’s edition. The Japanese title is その者。のちに…. Matching those names helps readers connect the anime announcement with the English books.

The manga also carries a longer Japanese subtitle referring to Wazu’s adventure and his unexpectedly S-rank strength. That extra wording identifies an edition or adaptation of the same property; it is not evidence of a separate anime season. When comparing listings, check the protagonist’s name, the original author and whether the page describes a novel, manga or television production.

What is the story about?

The anime’s advertised setup follows Wazu, a young man who leaves home and ends up on a mountain inhabited by powerful monsters. Living there with the small dragon Mear, he acquires extraordinary strength without fully appreciating how unusual he has become. His later travels mix overwhelming fighting ability with romantic misunderstandings.

Earth Star’s original-book description makes heartbreak the reason for his departure. The resulting contrast is the useful hook: physical strength does not automatically make Wazu successful at relationships. The series is being promoted as a fantasy adventure with romantic-comedy elements, rather than a story whose central problem is simply finding a stronger opponent.

This is a spoiler-light outline of the published premise, not a review of finished episodes. It does not establish which encounters the anime will adapt, how much time it will spend on the mountain, or where the season will finish. Readers who want to sample the storytelling before a trailer should use the official manga preview linked below.

Who voices Wazu and Mear?

Kento Ito voices Wazu, and Yui Ogura voices Mear. Both roles are named in the publisher’s November 22 cast announcement. Ito is also the performer announced for the opening theme, giving him separate acting and music roles in the project.

The pair’s functions are different: Wazu is the adventurer whose strength drives the premise, while Mear is the small dragon accompanying him. A two-person lead-cast announcement does not mean the entire series has only two voiced characters. Additional roles should be added when the production identifies them, rather than inferred from the number of people shown in the first promotional material.

Is the opening song already announced?

The opening performer is confirmed; the reviewed notices do not supply a song title or release date. Pony Canyon’s November 24 artist update confirms that Ito will play Wazu and perform the opening. It connects the reveal with his November 22 live event at Yokohama Bay Hall.

That update also discusses Ito’s recent music activities, but an artist’s existing EP is not automatically the anime’s theme-song release. Until a notice names the opening track, this guide will keep the music entry at “Kento Ito — title TBA.” A future single date should likewise be treated as a music release, not substituted for the television premiere.

Which studio is producing the anime?

Jumondou (寿門堂, also romanized Jumondo) is the announced animation studio. The publisher’s staff list names Koichi Ohata as chief director and Seung-Deok Kim as director. The original novels are by Nahato, with illustrations by Miya Kazutomo; the manga adaptation is credited to Shinichiro Nariie.

Those credits distinguish the television production from the books. They do not establish an episode count or guarantee that the anime will cover every published volume. When later promotional material arrives, compare any new adaptation details with the original announcement rather than assuming that the size of the book catalog determines the season’s length.

No streaming service or international territory list is established by the official announcements reviewed on November 24. The publisher confirms a TV anime and says broadcast details will come later. The available material does not support naming Crunchyroll, Netflix or another service as the confirmed viewing destination.

If you want to watch legally at launch, the next useful announcement is one that names both a platform and the countries it covers. An English manga license also does not determine animation distribution rights.

Where can you read The One. Later On… in English?

Titan Manga publishes an English edition of Volume 1. Its catalog lists a June 9, 2026 release and ISBN 9781787747432, with print buying options and a digital listing. The page includes a look-inside section, providing a way to sample the manga through the publisher.

Start with Volume 1 if you want the introduction to Wazu’s circumstances. A later volume may appear more prominently in store results because it is newer, but that is not a recommended starting point. Check the language, format and volume number before ordering; local availability and prices depend on the seller and country.

The June book date is separate from the 2027 anime window. It tells readers when that English manga edition was released, not when an episode became available. Similarly, a listing for a later book in January 2027 should not be read as confirmation that the anime will begin that month.

Is the source a light novel or a manga?

The original work is a novel, with a manga adaptation also published by Earth Star. The publisher’s announcement lists twelve main novel volumes and a side-story volume, alongside six manga volumes in Japan at the time of the reveal. Those are separate formats with separate reading sequences.

The English Titan listing verified here is for the manga. It should not be described as an English translation of the prose novels. Nor does the existence of twelve novels tell us that the television season will have twelve episodes or adapt one volume per episode. The production has not supplied that kind of coverage plan in the reviewed notices.
