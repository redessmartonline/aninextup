Why the second cour matters to the season

The October return is the second broadcast block of Ace of the Diamond act II -Second Season-, not a newly numbered third season. The season began in April 2026, and the October schedule resumes the same adaptation. Viewers who stopped after the spring episodes can continue with the next episode in sequence; there is no separate reboot or new cast announcement attached to this cour transition.

That distinction is useful when searching streaming services. Catalogs may keep the episodes under the existing season page and continue the episode numbering, instead of creating a new title for “Cour 2.” If a service appears not to list a new season, check the current act II -Second Season- page and its latest episode row before assuming the episodes are missing.

When does Cour 2 begin?

The second cour began in Japan on October 11, 2026. TV Tokyo’s six-station network schedules it for Sundays at 5:30 p.m. JST; AT-X lists a Sunday 9:00 p.m. JST broadcast with repeat showings. These are Japanese broadcast times. They should not be converted into a universal streaming time, because local services and time zones can differ.

The official series site says Netflix receives the episodes after the Japanese broadcast, with other listed services following. Its schedule includes ABEMA, DMM TV, d Anime Store, Disney+, FOD, Hulu, J:COM STREAM, Lemino, milplus and music.jp. The platform list is Japan-specific. Crunchyroll separately lists the title for supported international regions, but territory and episode availability should be checked in each viewer’s local catalog.

What the story is carrying forward

The continuing season follows Eijun Sawamura and Seido High as they pursue a place at Koshien. Sawamura’s move into the ace role gives the team a different pressure point: the position is not only a personal milestone but also a responsibility that affects how the whole pitching staff and defense prepare. The series’ appeal comes from showing that work through practice, selection and games rather than treating one successful performance as a complete arrival.

The October schedule therefore makes most sense as a continuation of the existing team arc. Returning viewers can track how Seido responds to its opponents and how Sawamura handles expectations around the ace number. This is a reading of the confirmed premise and character setup, not a claim about a particular game’s result.
