When does the ELECEED anime come out?

ELECEED is scheduled for April 2027. The production confirmed that month on October 21, 2026. Its English-language announcement describes a worldwide April premiere, while the Japanese notice confirms the broadcast month. Neither gives an exact first-episode date.

April is therefore the useful date to put on a watchlist. It does not mean April 1, and it does not establish a weekday or an episode release hour. Those details need a separate schedule announcement. This guide keeps the month separate from information that has yet to be published.

Where will ELECEED stream?

Crunchyroll is the previously announced streaming partner. Its distribution announcement is the platform source; the newer production notice supplies the April window. A streaming licence and a dated episode listing answer different questions, so viewers still need their local service schedule before planning an episode-one watch.

The English trailer is not an English dub release announcement. No dubbed launch date or English voice cast is established by the October 21 production notices. Likewise, “worldwide” in promotional wording should not be treated as a promise of identical subtitle choices, release hours or catalogue availability in every location. What is ELECEED about?

Jiwoo is a young man who hides his remarkable reflexes while quietly helping other people and animals. Taking in an injured cat introduces him to Kayden, a formidable fighter trapped in feline form. Their unlikely household opens the door to a hidden society of people with extraordinary abilities.

The appeal of that setup is the mismatch between the pair: Jiwoo's instinct is to help, while Kayden brings experience of a dangerous world that Jiwoo barely understands. The cat is central to their meeting and partnership, rather than an unrelated mascot. Newcomers can approach the series through that relationship without learning the wider cast in advance.

Why is Jiwoo called Yu Kazahaya on the anime website?

The official English trailer announcement calls the lead Jiwoo, matching the English WEBTOON synopsis. The Japanese anime site lists him as Yu Kazahaya. Readers moving between the two versions should expect different naming conventions rather than mistake the Japanese character listing for a different protagonist or a sequel.

For cast searches, use the names printed in the anime's own credits. For the English comic, start with the names on WEBTOON. This also helps avoid attaching a voice actor to a fan-translated character name without an official match. Related reading: Reincarnated as a Sword Season 2 English Dub Release Date on HIDIVE.

Who is in the Japanese cast, and which studio is making it?

The official production website lists Junya Enoki as Yu Kazahaya, Tasuku Hatanaka as Jin Tatsuoka, Setsuji Sato as Dr. Delein, Hayato Dojima as Sota Yanagi and Shizuka Ito as Amane Yanagi. Kayden's voice credit remains a question mark on the current site; this guide does not assign an actor to that role.

DandeLion Animation Studio produces the anime, with Hiroshi Nishikiori directing, Yousuke Kuroda handling scripts and series composition, and Minami Sakura designing the characters. These are production credits, not a guarantee of how many comic chapters will be adapted. An episode count and adaptation endpoint remain unannounced.

Where can you read the original ELECEED comic?

The English edition is on WEBTOON, credited to writer Jeho Son and artist ZHENA. Use the series page's “First episode” link to start at the beginning; its default list shows recent chapters, which can reveal later developments before a newcomer meets the characters.

Reading the comic is optional background, not a prerequisite for watching an adaptation whose episode coverage is still unknown. Anyone wanting to avoid story spoilers can stop after the opening introduction and wait for the anime. Readers continuing further should follow the official episode order rather than guess where a future television season might finish.

Is ELECEED a manga adaptation or a webtoon adaptation?

The source is a Korean webtoon, presented in English on WEBTOON as an action comic. That is the original reading option linked by the anime production, rather than a novel that viewers need to find first. The Japanese site credits the source writer as SJH, while WEBTOON uses Jeho Son; ZHENA is credited for the artwork on both. Those credit differences can make searches look inconsistent, but the official pages identify the same source work.
