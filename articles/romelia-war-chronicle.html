Romelia War Chronicle: Release Date, Story & Where to Watch

Romelia War Chronicle began streaming in Japan on October 3, 2026, at 9:00 p.m. JST, with Japanese television broadcasts starting October 5. International streaming began October 3; exact availability varies by region.

Release date, streaming start and Japanese TV schedule

Romelia War Chronicle began streaming in Japan on October 3, 2026, at 9:00 p.m. Japan Standard Time (JST). The official schedule lists Anime Times, U-NEXT and Netflix for that advance streaming launch. Japanese television followed on October 5, when TOKYO MX, BS11, Sun Television and KBS Kyoto began their broadcasts. CBC joined on October 10. These are separate release routes for the same TV anime, not different premieres.

The official schedule describes the television run as two cours. It lists TOKYO MX, BS11 and Sun Television on Mondays at 11:30 p.m. JST, KBS Kyoto on Mondays at midnight, and CBC on Saturdays at 2:43 a.m. in the broadcaster's extended schedule notation. CBC's first episode was listed for 2:58 a.m. Sunday, October 11, by ordinary calendar time. Japanese listings can show late-night hours above 24:00; check a local electronic program guide if the displayed day matters. The anime's official site warns that broadcast and streaming times may change.

Where can viewers outside Japan watch?

The production's official schedule lists Crunchyroll for international streaming from October 3, excluding Japan, mainland China and South Korea. For South Korea, it lists Tooniverse from the same date. The site does not give a universal release hour for those services, so the Japanese 9:00 p.m. start should not be copied as a global Crunchyroll time. Catalogues, subtitle availability and episode timing can vary by country; viewers should use the schedule shown by the service in their region. That distinction answers a common release-date question: October 3 is the announced Japan streaming date and the international streaming start date, but only the Japanese services have a precise 9:00 p.m. JST time on the official schedule. October 5 is the Japanese television start. A calendar entry saying “October 3” alone does not mean every territory received the episode at the same moment.

What is the story about?

The official introduction opens after Romelia and Prince Henri return from defeating the Demon King Zelgis. Henri ends his engagement to Romelia, while the celebrations around the victory overlook the damage left in her homeland. The country is still scarred by war, and people have lost homes and livelihoods during the conflict with the demons. Romelia chooses to return and address the problems that remain instead of treating the Demon King's defeat as a complete resolution.

Her stated approach includes organizing an army, restarting economic activity and navigating conspiracies. The official synopsis also warns that her decisions will bring the threat of another war. This makes “what happens after the Demon King is defeated?” a useful way to understand the premise: the story shifts from winning a quest to rebuilding the institutions and everyday life that war has disrupted. The anime's official material establishes those goals, but it does not promise that every plan succeeds or explain the later political outcomes. The broken engagement redirects Romelia's path, but the official premise gives similar weight to military preparation and reconstruction. That distinguishes the setup from a story focused only on courtship: it asks how a country might recover and how political conspiracies could complicate that work.

How many episodes or cours are confirmed?

The official Japanese broadcast page says the series will air for two cours. That confirms a longer run than a single cour, but the schedule page does not state an episode count, finale date, or whether a break separates the cours. Those details should not be inferred from the phrase alone. The same page gives weekly Japanese broadcast slots, which indicate the planned cadence, while a distributor's regional episode list remains the best source for local streaming dates. Who made the anime, and who is in the cast?

Atra is the animation studio. Koji Saito is series director, Takahiro Hirata assistant director, and Aya Satsuki is credited with series composition and scripts. Yoshiko Saito and Yuta Otaka design the characters, while KOHTA YAMAMOTO composes the score. The opening theme is “Akatsuki ni Naru” by Chogakusei; the ending is “Nightfall feat. Maika Loubté” by KOHTA YAMAMOTO.

Yuna Nemoto voices Romelia, Jin Ogasawara plays Al, Tomohiro Ono plays Ray, and Yuya Hirose plays Henri. The official cast page also lists additional characters including Elisabeth, Ekaterina, Ryoki and Gran. These credits help viewers distinguish confirmed production information from details that remain unannounced, such as later guest roles or episode-specific performers.

Is the anime based on a light novel or manga?

The official staff page credits the original work to Ryō Ariyama and identifies the source novel as a Gagaga Books publication from Shogakukan. It separately credits Ryo Ueto's manga adaptation, published in Mag Garden's BLADE Comics line. The anime therefore has both a credited novel source and a manga adaptation; the official credits do not say that the TV series adapts only the manga. Viewers who want to explore the story in print can use those publisher names to identify the relevant editions, while checking local book retailers for language and availability.
