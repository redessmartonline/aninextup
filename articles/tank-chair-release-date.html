When did TANK CHAIR premiere?

The television anime began in Japan on Sunday, October 4, 2026. Check your local service for the latest episode. Viewers comparing autumn listings can also use our Fall 2026 anime calendar. The official schedule gives a weekly broadcast pattern, while its late-night notation means some listings fall on the following calendar day. This distinction explains why two channels can both list the same Sunday program but show different dates on a calendar.

TOKYO MX lists new episodes Sundays at 11:00 p.m. Japan Standard Time (JST). BS11 lists Sundays at 24:30, which is 12:30 a.m. on Monday by the ordinary clock. CBC lists Mondays at 25:37, or 1:37 a.m. Tuesday, and MBS lists Tuesdays at 27:00, or 3:00 a.m. Wednesday. AT-X adds a Tuesday broadcast at 10:00 p.m. JST. Those are Japanese station times; viewers abroad should not convert them into a local streaming promise.

Where can you stream TANK CHAIR?

The official anime distribution notice says Prime Video starts advance streaming in Japan at 11:30 p.m. JST on Sundays, following the TOKYO MX broadcast. It also lists a later Wednesday rollout across other Japanese subscription video services, beginning October 7 at 11:30 p.m. JST. The order matters: a service listed for Wednesday is not necessarily carrying the episode at the same time as Prime Video, and the schedule refers to Japanese catalogs. For viewers outside Japan, the answer depends on country and service catalog. Kodansha USA’s September 15, 2026 title feature lists Crunchyroll for the anime’s October 2026 premiere, while the Japanese anime site separately documents Prime Video and other domestic platforms. Neither statement establishes that every service carries every episode in every territory. Check the current episode listing for your country, and use the Japanese broadcast times only as a reference for the original schedule.

What is the story about?

Nagi Taira is a highly capable assassin who falls into a coma after taking a bullet meant for his younger sister, Shizuka. She discovers a disturbing condition tied to his recovery: he can briefly awaken when he senses murderous intent. Shizuka then takes on dangerous jobs and brings Nagi into encounters with increasingly formidable opponents, hoping that a decisive battle will bring him back fully.

The official description frames this as a violent journey of recovery and a story about the siblings’ bond. The “rehabilitation” premise is deliberately extreme and belongs to the manga’s fictional action world; it is not medical guidance or a realistic account of coma care. The series also places the siblings in Guicheng Island, a dangerous city where gangs use genetic doping to gain power. That setting helps explain the unusual enemies and heightened combat stakes without requiring viewers to know the manga first. Related reading: Black Clover Season 2 Schedule & Where to Watch.

Who are Nagi, Shizuka and the Kurosaka siblings?

Koki Uchiyama voices Nagi, and Minori Fujidera voices Shizuka. Their central relationship gives the action a clear emotional question: whether Shizuka can help her brother recover and what risks she is willing to take. Gakuto Kajiwara plays Naozumi Kurosaka, Nagi’s former classmate and rival, while Saori Hayami plays Touko, Naozumi’s younger sister and battle partner.

The wider ensemble includes Yurina Amami as Dr. Radio, Gen Sato as Uzu, Megumi Ogata as Sensei, Nobuyuki Hiyama as Goro Arashiyama, Hochu Otsuka as Ryo Takanashi, and Tetsu Inada as Yoshitsune. These names come from the anime’s official cast listing.

Which studio and staff make the anime?

Polygon Pictures handles animation. Tadahiro “Tady” Yoshihira and Hiroaki Ando are both credited as directors, with Yoshihira also responsible for series composition and screenplay and Ando sharing screenplay duties. Yuki Moriyama designs the characters, Kazuma Teshigahara handles production design, and Masaru Yokoyama composes the music. The official staff page also lists separate art, color, sound, and CG supervision roles, useful context for a series built around elaborate machinery, a combat wheelchair, and a dense urban setting. The two-director credit is confirmed, but it should not be turned into an unsupported claim about which director handled a particular episode or visual sequence. Polygon Pictures describes the project as an action story centered on bloodlust and bonds. That production framing matches the unusual premise, while the on-screen execution and episode-by-episode division of work remain matters for the finished series and credits. Is TANK CHAIR based on a manga?

Yes. Manabu Yashiro created the ongoing manga published by Kodansha. Kodansha’s English-language series page describes its setting, Nagi’s injury, Shizuka’s discovery, and the dangerous opponents she seeks. That gives anime-only viewers a reliable starting point and gives manga readers a way to compare the adaptation’s setup with the source without assuming that the television series will cover a particular number of chapters.

The anime announcements confirm the adaptation and its creative team, but they do not state an episode count, a season-ending chapter, or whether every arc will be adapted. Readers deciding whether to start the manga can begin with volume one; viewers who prefer the anime can follow the broadcast and streaming listings above. What music appears in the series and trailer?

The August 2026 announcement names SiM’s “DALALA” as the opening theme and Daoko’s “SYNC” as the ending theme. The official site separately announced the digital release timing for the ending song. The main promotional video uses “DALALA” alongside action footage, while the character teaser focuses on voices and the central cast. This makes the main PV the better preview of the show’s pace and music, and the character video the more direct introduction to its performers.
