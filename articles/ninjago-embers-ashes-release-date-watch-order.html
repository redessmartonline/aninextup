NINJAGO Anime 2027: Embers + Ashes Release Window, Trailer and Watch Order

The new NINJAGO Legends: Embers + Ashes is coming in 2027, according to LEGO’s official teaser. March has been reported, while the exact premiere date and full-episode platform remain unconfirmed. The quickest catch-up is Duskfall, a three-short story you can watch now on LEGO’s website.

When does the new NINJAGO anime come out?

LEGO’s official teaser identifies the project as LEGO NINJAGO Legends: Embers + Ashes and gives it a 2027 release window. The video was uploaded on November 6, 2026. Coverage of the reveal points to March 2027, while LEGO’s YouTube description lists the year. The Brick Post reported March after the teaser release. The teaser itself closes with a coming-soon card, so treat March as reported until LEGO publishes a month or day in its own release information. The sources reviewed for this guide give no exact premiere day or episode-by-episode schedule.

Another date you may see is January 1, 2027. That is the shipping date shown on the U.S. LEGO Shop listing for Ivo’s Ninja Dojo, the building set connected to the show. It belongs to the product listing. For a viewing reminder, use the release window for the animated project and wait for the premiere listing to supply the exact day. Keeping those dates separate matters because the toy can ship before an episode is available to watch.

Watch the official Embers + Ashes teaser

The first teaser runs 1 minute and 18 seconds and comes from LEGO’s official YouTube channel. It introduces the new Legends chapter through short glimpses of the ninja, the city and their opponents. The search term “Ninjago anime” can also return footage from Duskfall or Monstrosity. Look for the Embers + Ashes title and the LEGO uploader to find the trailer for this particular project.

Open the official teaser on YouTube

Pay attention to the way the teaser uses movement and color to distinguish its characters before it tells you much about them. A red-clad ninja fills the official thumbnail against a dark city backdrop, with a bright sweep of fire drawing the eye across the frame. The emphasis on motion and contrast gives the preview a different feel from the product shots of the dojo. The set shows the location as a model you can explore. The teaser shows the visual tone the animated project is aiming for.

Where will Embers + Ashes stream?

YouTube is confirmed for the teaser. The LEGO materials checked for this guide do not identify a premiere platform or a release schedule for the full Embers + Ashes episodes. LEGO makes earlier Legends stories available online, which gives fans a place to catch up while the new chapter is still awaiting distribution details. Use the LEGO upload for the teaser and look for a separate official episode listing before making plans around any streaming service.

For parents or anyone looking for a short viewing session, there is an official option today. LEGO’s English-language NINJAGO hub has all three Duskfall shorts. They run about nine minutes altogether, so the catch-up fits into a single sitting. Each episode has its own page. Start at episode one even if your search results put the third episode or a compilation first.

What should you watch before Embers + Ashes?

Start with NINJAGO Legends: Duskfall. LEGO describes that story as a glimpse into the far future of NINJAGO. It is the closest lead-in to the new project, whose title and teaser both pick up the language of dusk, embers and a new beginning. The three shorts give you a feel for the era and the kind of danger its characters face without committing to a full season of the main show.

  1. Episode 1: The End Begins. A ninja interrupts an Oni ritual. This is the opening chapter to watch before the city sequence.
  2. Episode 2: Terminus. The focus moves to a citizen trying to escape through a city where monsters are fighting.
  3. Episode 3: Epilogue. Two elemental mech pilots are at the center of the closing short.

NINJAGO Legends: Monstrosity is another official Legends story, but it serves a different purpose in a watch order. LEGO places Kai’s time in the Land of Monsters during season one of Dragons Rising. If you want to explore Kai’s missing time or compare another entry in the Legends format, add it afterward. If your immediate goal is to prepare for the far-future story, Duskfall is the more direct starting point.

The title Dragons Rising belongs to a separate series. It is useful background for readers exploring the franchise more broadly, especially those following Kai. Starting the main show first is a larger commitment than watching the three Duskfall episodes. For a newcomer who only saw the Embers + Ashes trailer, the shorter route gives the most relevant context first.

Who are the new ninja and villains?

LEGO’s official listing for Ivo’s Ninja Dojo names Jin, Mira, Nate and Tre alongside Master Ivo and Master Zane. It also names the Oni Imperator and an Oni Warrior among the figures. This gives viewers an official reference for spellings while the series is still being introduced. LEGO’s Legends hub separately highlights Jin, Mira and Nate among the characters associated with the far-future setting.

The product page helps identify the dojo and the people LEGO chose to include in that set. The trailer is the better reference for how the show presents its world in motion. Treat the roster as a guide to names and the setting, while saving questions about episode roles, family connections and voice actors for fuller character announcements. A figure’s inclusion in one set gives very little away about the size of that character’s part across the series.

Is Embers + Ashes the same as the original NINJAGO show?

Embers + Ashes belongs to the NINJAGO Legends line, which LEGO uses to explore stories in different parts of the franchise’s timeline. LEGO described Duskfall as anime in its announcement for the Seoul premiere. That label helps explain why fans looking up the new Legends teaser use “Ninjago anime,” while the official title is the clearest way to find the right video and future release announcements.

LEGO’s shop calls Embers + Ashes a TV series in its product description. The November teaser introduces the project and its look, with fuller details about episode lengths, the production studio and the release format still to come in the sources reviewed here. If you are comparing it with the three very short Duskfall episodes, keep their running times separate until LEGO publishes an episode listing for the new chapter.

The simplest order is to watch the teaser, open the three Duskfall shorts in episode order, then return to the new series when LEGO announces how the episodes will be released. If you only have time for one catch-up story, make it Duskfall. If you also want to see how Legends can tell a story set during a main-series season, follow it with Kai’s Monstrosity.

How the NINJAGO Legends stories fit together

The easiest way to keep the three titles straight is by their starting points. Monstrosity follows a familiar ninja during a gap in an existing story. Duskfall jumps forward and introduces the future through several brief perspectives. Embers + Ashes picks up the future-facing Legends material with names that LEGO is already using in its character pages and its dojo listing. A viewer interested in release order can go from Monstrosity to Duskfall. A viewer interested specifically in the new project can start with Duskfall.

The two starting points also change how you read the teaser. After Monstrosity, the contrast is between Kai’s isolated journey and a trailer that uses a city skyline, multiple figures and a dojo to suggest a wider setting. After Duskfall, the useful comparison is how both previews of the future use the city and the Oni threat. That gives returning viewers something recognizable even as the new trailer keeps the central conflict largely under wraps.

Use the official NINJAGO Legends hub to distinguish the existing stories, and the English episode hub when you want to play them. If you bookmark a single page for the new series, use the official teaser as a reference for the name. Searching with the full subtitle will be more precise than searching for the entire franchise or for the word “anime” alone.
