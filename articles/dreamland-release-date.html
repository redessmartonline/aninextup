Dreamland Anime: Release Date, Episodes & Where to Watch

The anime has premiered, but “release date” depends on the country and platform. The official Japanese site lists the first television broadcast for Saturday, October 17, 2026 at 26:00. Written as a regular calendar time, that is Sunday, October 18 at 2:00 a.m. Japan Standard Time (JST). TV Asahi lists Episode 1 in its Sunday 2:00–2:30 a.m. slot. France’s Animation Digital Network (ADN) announced its streaming launch for October 17, with a new episode each week. These dates differ because the Japanese TV slot crosses midnight and the French platform has its own release calendar.

In Japan, the official anime site lists weekly streaming windows: Saturday late-night 3:00 a.m. for one group of services and Monday midnight for another. The latest episode is also available free for a limited time on ABEMA and TVer. The site links to U-NEXT, Animehodai, d-Anime Store, DMM TV, FOD, Hulu and other services. Check the listing in your app, since each platform can set its own delivery time and catalog conditions. Ellipse Animation lists ADN, Crunchyroll and France Télévisions among project partners, but a partner list is not a country-by-country availability map. For France, ADN has an explicit October 17 launch notice; elsewhere, check your local catalog rather than assume a universal release hour.

The story follows Terrence, a teenager whose mother died in a fire. He has feared flames ever since, and nightmares make him relive the loss. In the official synopsis, he eventually realizes he is dreaming and confronts that fear instead of running from it. The moment changes his role in the dream world: Terrence awakens as a Traveler, a Dreamer able to explore Dreamland, a dimension formed from humanity’s dreams and nightmares. The fantasy setting grows from a personal conflict rather than appearing as an unrelated portal adventure. Related reading: Cyberpunk: Edgerunners 2 Is Out on Netflix: Episodes & Trailer.

The official anime site adds that Terrence wants to return to Dreamland to see his mother again. Its character profile describes him as an awkward, unlucky but kind high-school student who often becomes the group’s mood-maker. That background gives new viewers a useful starting point, but the site does not disclose how the season resolves his goal. It also profiles Eve, Lydia, Nyti and other companions for viewers who want to learn character names before watching.

Dreamland is based on the French comic by Reno Lemaire. Ellipse Animation’s series page calls it a French manga and lists 21 volumes released and more than 550,000 copies sold in France. The production page describes the adaptation as a 10-episode first season, with 22 minutes per episode, 2D animation, and a mix of comedy, adventure and fantasy aimed at teens and adults. It does not state how many comic volumes the television season covers, so claims about its adaptation endpoint should wait for an official guide.

Ellipse Animation and La Chouette Compagnie produce the series. The producer credits Juan Pablo Machado and Johanna Celse as directors; TV Asahi’s Japanese schedule lists Machado and Joe Celse in its staff block. Jean-Luc Cano and Antoine Maurel handle series composition, Bénédicte Ciaravino designs the characters and Fabien Nataf composes the music. The Japanese version has a localization team as well as the original production staff, reflecting that this French-origin story is being presented to a Japanese television audience. The Japanese cast includes Kakeru Hatano as Terrence, Asami Tano as Eve, Kaede Hondo as Lydia, Takuya Eguchi as Savane and Hiroyuki Yoshino as Sabba. TV Asahi’s Episode 1 listing also names Takuya Masumoto, Serika Hiromatsu, Makoto Furukawa, Daisuke Namikawa and Masahiko Tanaka. The official anime site reported additional cast news on October 23, so its cast page may expand after the premiere. Japanese localization and original French voice performances are separate audio tracks; check which version your streaming service provides.

The Japanese-version opening is “movin’on” by Faulieu., and the ending is “dream catcher” by Ai Tomioka. The official site lists both performers and identifies the songs as written for Dreamland. The official teaser on Crunchyroll’s verified YouTube channel offers a short introduction to the series, but promotional footage does not confirm which scenes appear in later episodes. Crunchyroll published its official teaser on July 12, 2026 at 05:15:15 PDT (UTC−07:00), equivalent to 12:15:15 UTC. The original YouTube page declares that timestamp in its upload metadata. The teaser introduces the series; it does not establish a single streaming hour for every region.

For a quick check: Japan’s Episode 1 was scheduled for October 18 at 2:00 a.m. JST, listed as October 17 at 26:00; ADN’s French streaming launch was October 17. Season 1 has ten 22-minute episodes. Platform partners and local schedules vary, while a second season and the portion of the comic adapted remain unannounced in the official sources reviewed here.
