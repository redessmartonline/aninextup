When did Made in Abyss: Awakening Mystery release?

The first installment in the Made in Abyss theatrical series opened in Japanese theaters on October 23, 2026. The date is a Japan release, not a worldwide premiere. The official Japanese title is Gekijō Series Dai-ichi-bu: Made in Abyss — Mezamuru Shinpi (劇場シリーズ【第一部】メイドインアビス 目覚める神秘). “Awakening Mystery” is a useful English rendering of the subtitle; the official film pages reviewed use the Japanese title rather than announcing an English-language title.

For viewers outside Japan, no international cinema date, regional distributor, or streaming launch appears in the official sources checked on October 25. A Japanese theatrical opening does not establish when the film will reach another country or platform. Check local distributors and theater listings for a region-specific announcement instead of treating October 23 as a global date.

Made in Abyss Part 2 is confirmed, but undated

On October 23, the official film site confirmed production of Part 2, describing it as the continuation of Part 1. The announcement also revealed a second key visual featuring the Curse Fleet, Hail Hex, led by Srajo. This is the clearest answer for readers asking whether the theatrical project will continue: yes, another installment is in production.

The announcement did not give Part 2 a release date, production schedule, runtime, distributor, or streaming plan. It did not publish a separate staff or cast list for the new installment. Part 1’s confirmed credits therefore should not automatically be presented as Part 2 credits. The project has established that the next film is being made, while its timing and production details remain unknown.

What is the first film about?

The official introduction recaps the journey from the sixth layer of the Abyss and points toward the seventh. Riko, Reg, Nanachi, and Faputa leave the Village of the Hollows behind after its collapse and continue deeper into the unknown. The setup follows the events of the second television season; the film is a continuation of that journey, not a remake of the original series or an announced third TV season.

The official introduction includes significant story detail from earlier chapters, including Prushka’s transformation into Riko’s White Whistle and the fate of the village. New viewers who want to avoid those reveals can begin with the television seasons and consult the film’s synopsis afterward. The project site does not describe the full plot of Part 1 or explain how many manga chapters it adapts, so a precise adaptation breakdown is not confirmed.

Who made the film and who returns?

The official credits list Akihito Tsukushi as the original creator, Masayuki Kojima as director, Hideyuki Kurata as screenwriter, and Kinema Citrus as the animation studio. Character design is credited to Yuka Kuroda and Kazuya Kise, with Kevin Penkin composing the music. These familiar series credits connect the film to the existing anime, but the official page identifies this project as the first part of a theatrical series.

The principal returning voices include Miyu Tomita as Riko, Mariya Ise as Reg, Shiori Izawa as Nanachi, and Misaki Kuno as Faputa. The film also lists Sumire Morohoshi as Tepaste, Takanori Hoshino as Clavari, Nana Mizuki as Srajo, Akino Watanabe as Nishagora, Shunsuke Takeuchi as Yataramaru, Nao Toyama as Sherumi, and Yume Miyamoto as Menae. Those names are confirmed for Part 1; a Part 2 cast announcement has not been made.

Official trailer and upload date

KADOKAWAanime’s verified YouTube page identifies the official main trailer and displays August 6, 2026 as its publication date. The page does not display an exact upload time or timezone, so this guide reports only the date and does not infer a time or convert it to Japan time. The official movie page links the video directly.

The trailer and official introduction establish the project’s title, release, and story context. Promotional footage should not be treated as confirmation of scenes or plot points that the production has not described in its written synopsis.

Can Japanese theatergoers still receive the opening bonus?

The official first-week campaign runs from October 23 through October 30, 2026. Participating screenings include a short Hime-sama Nikki mini-anime after the feature, and visitors receive one of two Akihito Tsukushi illustrated mini shikishi designs at random. The mini-anime expands an April Fools’ Day illustration into a short screening bonus. The campaign is specific to the listed Japanese opening week; check with the individual cinema for participation and current bonus availability.

Because the campaign is time-limited, check the theater before traveling. The official notice says the mini-anime runs after the feature and the printed illustration is distributed at random; neither item is a separate episode release or a Part 2 date announcement.

Where can viewers watch it now?

As of October 25, the confirmed release information covers Japanese theaters. The official pages reviewed do not confirm overseas screenings, a home-video date, or a streaming service. The same pages confirm Part 2 production but do not provide a release window. Readers looking for a legal option outside Japan should wait for a local distributor or platform to announce availability; no service should be named as confirmed on the basis of the Japanese cinema opening alone.

For the most current primary information, follow the official theatrical-series site, its October 23 Part 2 announcement, the Part 1 introduction, and the official staff and cast page. For bonus terms, consult the first-week theater notice; for the trailer, use the official video page.
