Bocchi the Rock! Starry Footprints Movie: 2027 Release, Teaser & Story

Bocchi the Rock! Hoshi no Ashiato, also called Starry Footprints in English coverage, opens in Japanese cinemas in 2027. The new prequel follows Seika and Nijika Ijichi before Kessoku Band forms, adapting a story from manga volume five. An official teaser and visual are available; an exact date and international release remain unannounced.

When is the Bocchi the Rock! prequel movie coming out?

The confirmed window is 2027 in Japanese theaters. Aniplex announced the project on November 1, 2026, releasing a teaser visual and a short promotional video. There is no confirmed month or day in that announcement. November 6 is the start of the first Japanese advance-ticket sale, not the film's opening date.

The Japanese title is Eiga Bocchi the Rock! Hoshi no Ashiato. English-language coverage has used Starry Footprints for the subtitle; the production website identifies the film as Hoshi no Ashiato. That distinction is useful when searching theater listings later: a licensed international title may be announced separately. For now, the Japanese title is the most reliable identifier, especially alongside the older compilation films.

The announcement also names a Japanese distributor, ANIMEC. It does not establish an overseas opening, an English dub or a streaming destination. If you are planning around a local cinema release, the next useful announcement will be from a distributor naming your country, rather than another page repeating the Japanese year.

Whose story does Hoshi no Ashiato tell?

The focus moves to sisters Seika and Nijika Ijichi before Kessoku Band forms. Seika is familiar to television viewers as STARRY's manager; Nijika is the drummer who brings Hitori Gotoh into the band. The film's official introduction frames this earlier story around the sisters' dreams and a farewell. It is a look behind the circumstances that make the main series possible.

That shift is the interesting part of the announcement. The television introduction begins with Hitori practicing alone and wanting somewhere to belong. A story centered on the Ijichi sisters can turn our attention toward the people and places that make belonging possible. Instead of asking only how someone joins a band, it invites a question about how a space for that band comes to exist. That is our reading of the premise, rather than a claim about scenes not yet released.

There is no need to explain the sisters' entire past to understand the pitch. Keeping the farewell's circumstances out of a release guide preserves the discovery for new viewers. What matters here is the change of perspective: characters who help support Hitori's story become the people whose own hopes need room on screen.

How is it different from Season 2 and the compilation films?

Three projects can easily get mixed together in search results. The 2024 theatrical compilations revisit the television story. The second TV season is a separate announced production. Hoshi no Ashiato adapts an earlier story about the Ijichi sisters. Its 2027 cinema window should not be copied into a Season 2 schedule.

The official film website acknowledges both the earlier compilations and the second season. Screenwriter Erika Yoshida also discusses the film and Season 2 as separate work in her accompanying comment. Neither the movie's year nor its advance-ticket notice gives us a release order for the two new productions. We should wait for dated announcements before claiming which arrives first.

For a newcomer, our suggested starting point remains the first television season. It introduces Hitori, Nijika, the band and the performance space through their everyday interactions. Then the prequel can offer another angle on people you already know. This is an editorial viewing suggestion, not an official prerequisite: the creators' comments also welcome people encountering the franchise through the movie.

What the first visual tells us

The official image places the younger sisters together in the back of a car. A guitar case rises beside them, while Nijika holds a small frog toy. The confined setting makes their physical closeness immediately readable. Music is present as an object they carry, rather than as a finished performance in front of an audience.

Our interpretation is that this is a strong choice for a prequel about dreams. A stage image would sell the destination; this picture sells the journey toward it. The guitar case and the toy also hold two stages of growing up within the same frame. Neither detail tells us how the story resolves, but both give the announcement a more intimate point of entry than another ensemble concert pose.

For returning viewers, that changes the question attached to STARRY. It is easy to think of a live house as the backdrop where the band's story happens. Centering its manager and her sister asks us to consider the personal history behind that backdrop. The appeal is not simply additional franchise history; it is the possibility of seeing a familiar place acquire a different emotional weight.

Who is making the film?

Shin Wakabayashi directs, Erika Yoshida writes the screenplay and Yukiko Horiguchi handles character design. CloverWorks produces the animation. Maaya Uchida voices Seika Ijichi, and Sayumi Suzushiro voices Nijika. Those two roles are the announced center of this film; a television ensemble listing should not be treated as a complete movie cast announcement.

The production comments offer a more useful clue than a prediction based on studio reputation. Aki Hamazi explains that the original story grew from a wish to expand the sisters' past seen near the end of television episode eight, tracing the path toward STARRY. Hamazi also says Yoshida has expanded that material for the film. That confirms adaptation work beyond simply putting manga panels on screen, while leaving the actual additions undisclosed.

Wakabayashi's comment emphasizes wishes and their effect on the sisters. Read alongside Hamazi's account, it suggests a creative interest in what sustains a dream before it becomes a visible achievement. The finished film will have to earn that idea through its scenes; the comments give us a reason to watch for it, not grounds to review a movie nobody has seen.

Which manga volume contains the source story?

The official introduction identifies the material at the end of Aki Hamazi's fifth manga volume. Yoshida names the one-shot Hoshi ni Tamukeru Ai no Hana in her comment. This does not mean the film adapts the whole fifth volume, nor does it announce where the next television season will stop.

English readers can find Bocchi the Rock!, Vol. 5 through Yen Press, which lists paperback and digital editions released on November 26, 2024. Its catalogue synopsis describes the volume's main band storyline, so it should not be mistaken for the movie's synopsis. Use the volume number and publisher when locating the book; anthology volumes are a different publication line.

If you want to preserve the movie's surprises, wait rather than read detailed summaries of the one-shot. If you want to compare adaptations, keep the distinction between the short source story and the surrounding volume in mind. The creators have described an expansion, so matching page count to runtime would tell us little. No runtime has been confirmed in the release material reviewed here.

Tickets, concerts and the next release details to watch

The first Japanese advance tickets go on sale November 6, 2026, priced at ¥1,600 each. Their designs feature new illustrations of Seika and Nijika by Horiguchi, with matching stickers available while supplies last. These are advance cinema tickets for Japan. The sale does not turn November 6 into a premiere date or establish access for viewers overseas.

The same day as the film announcement, Kessoku Band also revealed its 2027 Asia tour: Osaka on March 13–14, Seoul on May 8 and Hong Kong on May 22. Those are concert dates, not film screenings. Readers following both announcements should keep the two schedules separate rather than use the tour as evidence for a theatrical opening.

For this guide, the next meaningful updates are an exact Japanese opening date, a fuller trailer, and country-specific distribution. International streaming remains unannounced in the sources checked.
