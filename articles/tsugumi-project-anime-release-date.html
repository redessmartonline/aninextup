When does the Tsugumi Project anime come out?

Tsugumi Project is scheduled to begin airing in Japan in January 2027. The anime’s official site confirmed the month on November 12, 2026, when it revealed the main key visual. The announcement gives the month for the Japanese television broadcast. The premiere day, time slot and international streaming plans remain open. January is the date range to keep on your watchlist while you wait for an episode schedule.

The anime appears in Japanese listings as 虎鶫, followed by Tsugumi Project. You may also see it called Tora Tsugumi or Toratsugumi. Kodansha uses Tsugumi Project for the English manga, so add “anime” when searching for a broadcast update and “ippatu” when looking for the book. The shared title makes it easy to mistake a volume’s publication date for a new anime announcement.

The November visual shows the characters against high-rise buildings partly reclaimed by vegetation. It is the main artwork for the adaptation and comes with the new January broadcast window. The project already had a teaser from its initial announcement. If you encounter the older teaser on its own, use the current official news page to confirm the broadcast month.

Where will Tsugumi Project stream?

The announcement confirms a TV anime in Japan. It does not identify a streaming service, international territories, an English dub or an English subtitle schedule. Crunchyroll News has covered the adaptation and cast, but coverage in its news section is distinct from a streaming announcement. Look for a title-specific streaming announcement or a series page in your territory when a distributor makes that information available.

If you are planning a watchlist, keep the Japanese premiere window and your local availability as separate fields. A Japanese broadcast update can provide an exact date before an overseas service publishes its schedule. Once a service confirms the series, check whether it names your country and whether the time shown refers to a subtitled or dubbed episode. What is Tsugumi Project about?

Leon is a former soldier taken from his family after a false conviction. His chance at freedom is a mission into the territory once known as Japan. He and other prisoners are sent to find a weapon known by a codename. The place they enter has been isolated for generations, and what they encounter there makes the mission far less straightforward than its briefing suggests.

The setting gives the story two immediate questions. Can the people sent on the mission survive the ruins, and how much do they really know about the thing they have been ordered to retrieve? An assignment that looks simple on paper becomes a problem of interpreting a place that has changed beyond recognition. Buildings are still visible, but the creatures and people living among them have adapted to a world the expedition barely understands.

Tsugumi, a girl with birdlike legs and extraordinary strength, is at home in that environment. Her ability to navigate it makes her a useful point of comparison with Leon, whose training comes from elsewhere. The animal Tora appears alongside her in the main visual. The setup invites viewers to consider what Tsugumi knows about the territory, what the mission organizers are keeping to themselves and why a weapon is worth sending prisoners into the ruins to find.

The manga’s opening is a way to sample the tone before the show begins. Kodansha classifies the book as science fiction and action and gives it a 16+ rating. Its sense of danger comes from more than the creatures: Leon has family waiting for him, and every decision he makes in Japan has consequences for whether he can get back. That is a useful starting point for readers who want a survival story with a personal reason to keep going.

Mizunaka plays Leon, whose military experience helps him survive the initial mission. Watada voices Tsugumi, whose knowledge and strength distinguish her from the newcomers. Ishiya plays Doudou, a fellow prisoner who encounters Leon in Old Japan. The production site presents the three profiles separately, which helps match the names to the figures in the visual. The official English art uses Léon, while Kodansha’s English synopsis spells the name Leon.

The production site also links voiced comic material. That can be a useful way to hear performances, particularly if you want to identify a character before the broadcast. These clips present manga material with recorded voices. The separately labeled anime teaser is the place to start if you want to see the footage used to promote the television adaptation.

Where can you read the Tsugumi Project manga in English?

Start with Tsugumi Project Volume 1 from Kodansha. The English series page lists print and digital formats, a free chapter preview and seven numbered books. The preview is a convenient way to see ippatu’s artwork and read the setup before choosing an edition. The product page for each volume has its own retailer and format information, so use the volume number when comparing listings.

If you are buying the book in print, use the English publisher listing to verify the language. The series was originally published by Ki-oon in France before its serialization in Kodansha’s Weekly Young Magazine. The French, Japanese and English books share much of their title. Checking the publisher and language is therefore more reliable than identifying the edition by a cover image alone.

Read the numbered volumes in order. The first introduces Leon’s assignment and the territory the team has entered. Later volume descriptions discuss developments from farther into the story, so stick to the first-volume listing if you want to avoid spoilers. The publisher’s series page gathers the volumes in one place, which is useful when you finish one and want to verify that you are opening the next.

If you plan to watch the anime first, you can save the English Volume 1 listing for afterward. The production has not said where the TV adaptation will stop in the source material, so there is no confirmed volume to jump to after a first season. Start with Volume 1 if you want to see how the original manga introduces the cast and shows the transition into Old Japan.

Watch the official Tsugumi Project teaser

The teaser on the anime’s website is the preview released with the adaptation announcement. NBCUniversal Anime/Music hosts the upload. The November announcement adds the main visual and the broadcast month. Use the teaser to get a sense of the adaptation’s presentation and the dated announcement for the current schedule.

The main visual is also useful for putting names to faces. Leon and Doudou are the men shown among the ruined buildings. Tsugumi is the girl with birdlike legs, and Tora is the large animal. That image tells you who is featured in the promotion while leaving their first encounters for the manga or the eventual episodes.

If you want one starting point today, open the official teaser for the anime or the publisher’s free preview for the manga. Those two links lead directly to material you can use before the January broadcast.
