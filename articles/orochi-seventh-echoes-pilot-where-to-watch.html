Where can you watch the OROCHI -Seventh Echoes- pilot?

Watch the 2-minute, 18-second pilot for free on the official BLUE RIGHTS YouTube channel. The producer released it on November 13, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. Japan Standard Time. The player below uses the exact upload linked in the production announcement. You can also open the video directly on YouTube to use its playback and caption controls.

The video title contains both the Japanese and English names, followed by the words Pilot Film. Look for that label and the uploader name, BLUE RIGHTS channel, if you search from a television or phone. The full title matters because Orochi appears in other Japanese films, games and stories. Adding Seventh Echoes gets you closer to this particular production.

Open the official pilot on YouTube.

The announced launch time was 01:00 UTC on November 13. In Santo Domingo, that was 9:00 p.m. on November 12. The difference explains why an official Japanese announcement and a video page in another time zone can show different dates. Both refer to the same release. Viewers arriving after launch can open the upload without waiting for another scheduled screening.

Is the full OROCHI movie out?

The full feature has no announced theatrical or streaming release date. BLUE RIGHTS describes the current upload as a pilot for an original feature-length film. The company also says the finished movie may differ from the pilot in its story details, presentation and designs. The YouTube release date applies to the preview that is already public.

That makes the pilot a useful way to decide whether you want to follow the project. You can see how the team introduces its world and the conflict around Agni, and hear the sound and music used in the preview. Questions about how those scenes fit into a completed film will have to wait until the production shares more. An international distributor, a wider theatrical rollout and a platform for the feature have yet to be announced in the release materials checked for this guide.

If you see a runtime attached to a festival pitch or a development listing, check what the listing describes before using it as the length of the finished release. A pitch identifies a project being presented to an industry audience. A ticket or streaming listing tells you when a completed version is scheduled for viewers. For now, the measurable running time is the 2:18 shown on the official pilot itself.

What is OROCHI -Seventh Echoes- about?

Agni grows up away from the royal family after being abandoned. He carries an unusual power called Hizumi and lives in Kukulkan, a kingdom sustained by sound. His power eventually awakens Orochi, a threat from the distant past. The encounter raises a question Agni has little reason to expect: what connects him to the being that has just returned?

The setup puts Agni in an awkward position before the larger conflict begins. The people who raised him form the life he knows, while the power he was born with links him to a history he never learned. If he wants to understand what just happened, he has to look back at decisions made long before he had any say in them. That gives the fantasy a personal question alongside its larger threat.

Sound is part of the premise, so the pilot is worth watching with the audio on. In a short clip it can be easy to focus on the creature designs and the scale of the action. Listening as well makes it easier to notice how the preview introduces a world built around vibrations and how the sound changes when that order is disrupted. The project’s setup gives that choice a story function, even though the producer has left the rules of the world for a fuller reveal.

Who is making the OROCHI anime movie?

Takashi Nakamura is credited for the original concept, direction, character design and art design. BLUE RIGHTS is producing the film, and the credits name Makoto Kimura as producer. Nakamura’s previous work includes Fantastic Children and A Tree of Palme. He also drew the teaser visual released with the pilot announcement. The preview and the artwork give you two ways to see his approach to the project.

The pilot features the theme song Zankyo, performed by neNna. The song credit is useful when you want to identify what you heard in the clip. The credited track accompanies the material BLUE RIGHTS has released so far. The completed movie’s music and release details may be described in more depth in later announcements.

The project was presented through Global Anime Challenge at Annecy’s 2026 Partners Pitches. That appearance helps explain why older reports discuss the film even though the pilot only went public in November. The pitch and the YouTube upload were two stages of the same project. If you are looking for a public screening of the feature, use future theatrical or distribution announcements for a date and venue.

Does the pilot have English subtitles?

The YouTube upload showed a Japanese automatic caption track when checked on November 13. A separate official English subtitle track was not listed. On devices that offer it, YouTube may let you choose Auto-translate and then English from the caption menu. That creates a machine translation from the available captions, so names and invented terms such as Hizumi may come through differently from the producer’s descriptions.

If the caption menu is limited in an embedded player, use the direct YouTube link and check the settings there. For a spoiler-light English starting point, use the premise above and then watch the clip. If you want to send it to a friend, link to the producer’s upload so they can see the latest title, description and caption options in one place.

What does the pilot show about the project?

In a little over two minutes, the team introduces a setting where sound matters and a protagonist whose power has consequences he cannot yet explain. That is enough to establish the kind of conflict the feature will explore. The story can then spend more time on the people who brought Agni up and on the kingdom’s relationship with Orochi. Those are threads the producer’s synopsis raises, and the pilot gives viewers a first impression of them.

If you are coming to the clip through Nakamura’s earlier films, start by looking at the character silhouettes and the environments around them. The same director is credited with shaping both for this project. The illustrated teaser poster provides another look at the design work, while the pilot shows how those choices read in movement. Together they offer a clearer introduction than a release-date listing alone.
