The Dangers in My Heart Season 3: April 2027 Release & Watch Order

The Dangers in My Heart Season 3 premieres on Japanese TV in April 2027. The new teaser picks up after Season 2. The exact premiere date and international streaming service are still pending.

When does The Dangers in My Heart Season 3 come out?

The Dangers in My Heart Season 3 starts in April 2027 in Japan. The official anime announcement, dated November 6 in Japan, narrows the earlier 2027 window to a month and comes with a teaser trailer and visual. It gives viewers a spring premiere window. The first episode’s date, broadcast hour and weekly schedule are still pending.

April is the Japanese television window. If you are watching from the United States, the United Kingdom or another market, the practical question is when a service in your country will carry the season. The announcement does not specify that service or say how its schedule would line up with Japanese television. A local episode time needs both pieces of information before it can be calculated.

Where will Season 3 stream?

International streaming for Season 3 has not been confirmed in the April announcement. The official on-air page is the place to check for Japanese broadcast and distribution details. That page also lists older streaming offers. If you see a service there, check which season the listing covers before using it to plan your spring viewing.

There are three distinct details to look for in a streaming announcement: the season number, your territory and the start date. A service might carry earlier episodes in one country while leaving the new season unlisted. Audio and subtitles can have their own schedules too. If you are specifically waiting for an English dub, look for a separate dub announcement or a title page that names the language for Season 3.

For now, bookmark the official schedule and check your local catalog for Seasons 1 and 2. You can also use the AniNextUp streaming guide to find other series by platform while the spring lineup takes shape.

What the Season 3 teaser shows

The teaser revisits Kyotaro Ichikawa and Anna Yamada after the turning point at the end of Season 2. The new visual places them together at a park at sunset. It gives the announcement a familiar setting and lets the change in their relationship do the work: the next stretch is about what they do now that they know how the other person feels.

Classmate Moeko Sekine appears in the preview, as do Kyotaro’s sister Kana and her bandmate Akira Momoyama. Those glimpses put friends and family around the central couple. The scenes are a useful indication of which relationships the production wants to show off, but they do not establish a complete arc list or how far the anime will adapt the manga.

Finish Season 2 before playing the teaser if you want to avoid spoilers about the finale.

What to watch before Season 3

  1. Season 1. Start here if you are new to the series. The early episodes show how Kyotaro’s private assumptions about Anna change as they spend time together, giving the later relationship its context.
  2. Season 2. Finish this season before jumping into the new episodes. The third-season announcement refers directly to the relationship established in its finale.
  3. The 2026 movie, if you want more. The film recuts the television story through Kyotaro’s perspective and adds animated scenes set after Season 2. Watch it after the two seasons if you want both the full television buildup and the extra material.

The film opened in Japan on February 13, 2026, according to Shin-Ei Animation. Its official announcement describes new footage that includes Kana’s live performance and another moment involving Kyotaro and Anna. Those scenes make the movie more than a straight compilation. The Season 3 announcement does not make it a prerequisite.

If you have time for one rewatch, start with the latter part of Season 2 and finish at the finale. If you watched only the movie, the TV series offers more room for the small school and family scenes that build up to the couple’s decisions. There is no need to put the film between the first and second seasons.

Can you read the manga while waiting?

Yes. Seven Seas Entertainment publishes Norio Sakurai’s original manga in English. Volume 1 is a straightforward starting point if you want to compare the story from the beginning. If you want to resume exactly where the television series left off, check the contents against the last events you remember before buying a volume. The teaser does not announce a starting chapter or an ending volume for Season 3.

There is also a separately licensed spin-off, The Dangers in My Heart: The Romantic Comedy Won’t Start, which follows Kana. Seven Seas lists its first English volume for January 2027. That date belongs to the spin-off manga. The April date belongs to the main television anime. If you are searching a bookstore, keep the subtitle in your search so you do not order the wrong series.

Season 3 questions

What title should I search for in a streaming app?

Search for The Dangers in My Heart first. The official Japanese website uses Boku no Kokoro no Yabai Yatsu, which is also shortened to BokuYaba. Open the season selector on the series page and check for Season 3. The movie can appear as a separate title, so a movie listing alone will not tell you whether the platform carries the new television episodes.

How many episodes will Season 3 have?

The announcement does not provide a total. Earlier seasons and the compilation movie have their own runtimes and release formats, which cannot establish the episode count for the new season.

Does the trailer confirm that Kana’s band has a full story arc?

The announcement identifies Kana and Akira in the teaser. It does not name a dedicated band arc or give a list of episodes. If you want more of Kana before Season 3, the movie’s new concert scene and her separately licensed manga spin-off are distinct options.

Where can I check other April anime premieres?

Use the AniNextUp release calendar to compare April titles. It distinguishes releases with a specific date from projects that currently have only a month or a broader window.

A catch-up plan for different viewers

If you remember the ending but little else: revisit the scenes where Kyotaro and Anna move from private conversations to making choices in front of other people. The trailer’s emphasis on classmates and Kana makes that transition a useful thread to follow. A rewatch with that focus gives you more context than a plot summary that only lists when the characters meet or confess.

If you found the anime through the movie: decide whether you want to see the relationship unfold at television pace before April. The film is edited around Kyotaro’s point of view. The original episodes offer more space between the major scenes, including everyday conversations that can feel minor on their own but accumulate across a season. The movie’s new scenes are a reason to keep it in your viewing order if you enjoy seeing what happens around the edges of the TV finale.

If you are starting completely fresh: avoid season-three trailer breakdowns until after the second season. Watch the first episode of Season 1 and give the series time to show the difference between Kyotaro’s narration and what he actually does. The official manga description presents that mismatch as part of the comedy. You can use the original English manga as a parallel read or save it for later, depending on how much of the story you want to encounter before the new episodes.
