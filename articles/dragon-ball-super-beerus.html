Who might want to watch the enhanced edition?

Dragon Ball Super: Beerus revisits established material with added and redrawn footage and a reconstructed narrative, according to the official description. That makes it relevant both to newcomers looking for this version of the story and to returning viewers interested in how familiar scenes have been reworked. The enhanced-edition label alone does not establish that every sequence is new.

What the enhanced edition means for viewers

The Beerus release is presented as an enhanced edition of the earlier Dragon Ball Super material, rather than a wholly unrelated Dragon Ball storyline. That distinction matters when deciding whether to watch it as a first introduction or revisit familiar events. The Japanese television debut and the shortly later Japanese streaming window are also separate release steps, not contradictory schedules.

Dragon Ball Super: Beerus premieres in Japan on October 11, 2026, at 11:15 p.m. Japan Standard Time. The official Dragon Ball pre-broadcast guide published October 9 confirms the broadcast time and the first Japanese streaming services. This is the ENHANCED edition of Dragon Ball Super, with newly added and redrawn footage and a reconstructed narrative, according to the official New York Comic Con report.

Should returning fans expect an entirely new story?

The official description calls Dragon Ball Super: Beerus an enhanced edition with additional and redrawn material and a reconstructed narrative. That signals a revised presentation of familiar events, not a confirmation of an unrelated sequel storyline. Returning viewers may want to compare how the Beerus material is presented, while newcomers can use the edition as an entry point to this part of the franchise. The announcements do not provide a scene-by-scene inventory of what has changed.

Release date

The Japanese premiere is scheduled for Sunday, October 11 at 11:15 p.m. JST. The first episode was screened in advance at New York Comic Con on October 8. The official report says the screening drew a crowd of approximately 3,000 attendees, with English dub cast members Jason Douglas (Beerus) and Ian Sinclair (Whis) taking part in the panel. This screening was a preview event, not a public streaming launch.

What is confirmed?

Akira Toriyama is credited for the original work, story and character design. The announced opening theme is “ONOGAMI” by MEGATERA・ZERO, while VK Blanka performs the ending theme.

Where to watch

Japan: The official pre-broadcast guide lists Prime Video and U-NEXT as the fastest streaming options, starting October 12 at 12:15 a.m. JST, after the October 11 television broadcast. Other Japanese streaming services are scheduled to follow.

Outside Japan: Crunchyroll's Fall 2026 lineup lists the series for supported international territories. The October 9 official Dragon Ball report left North American timing for a separate announcement. The primary Crunchyroll Episode 1 record identifies “The God Who Destroys” as released on October 11, 2026, with Japanese audio and Latin American Spanish, English and Brazilian Portuguese subtitles. We use that episode record for the premiere date. The October 12–18 weekly news calendar also listed Episode 1 on October 18; that conflicting calendar entry does not replace the date on the episode itself. A catalog release date does not guarantee playback in every territory.

Regional platform update: The official Dragon Ball Super: Beerus website now lists Hulu for the United States, Disney+ for Canada and Latin America, and Crunchyroll and Netflix under worldwide streaming services. These listings identify announced distribution partners; they do not by themselves establish identical episode release times across every territory. Disney’s Spanish press office separately lists an October 14 Disney+ premiere for Spain. Viewers should use the platform schedule for their own country rather than treating the Japanese October 11 broadcast time as a global streaming time. Why this update matters

The new official update adds an exact Japanese broadcast time, named Japanese streaming platforms and the first on-demand availability window. It also makes the international timing caveat important: a Japanese broadcast date does not guarantee simultaneous streaming in every region. The October 8 New York Comic Con screening confirms that Episode 1 has already received a preview, but does not establish a general release time outside Japan.
