Dragon Ball Super: Beerus English Dub Release Date & Schedule

The Dragon Ball Super: Beerus English dub premieres on Thursday, November 12, 2026, at 8 a.m. PT / 11 a.m. ET on Crunchyroll. New dubbed episodes will follow weekly. The launch covers the United States, Canada, Latin America, Australia and New Zealand. Spanish and Portuguese dubs start at the same time.

When does the English dub come out?

The English dub of Dragon Ball Super: Beerus starts Thursday, November 12, 2026, at 8 a.m. Pacific on Crunchyroll. The announcement says dubbed episodes will follow weekly. It gives a precise time for the launch, so use that time for the first release and check the series listing for the times of later episodes. The same announcement covers the Spanish and Portuguese dubs.

For the United States, the premiere converts to 9 a.m. Mountain, 10 a.m. Central and 11 a.m. Eastern. In the Dominican Republic it is noon on Thursday, and in Brasília it is 1 p.m. on Thursday. The calendar date changes across the Pacific: the same moment is 3 a.m. Friday, November 13, in Sydney and 5 a.m. Friday in Auckland. This is why a North American headline can say November 12 while an Australian one gives November 13. The times describe a single launch, with local clocks already on different dates.

The Japanese-audio simulcast follows a separate schedule. Crunchyroll’s season lineup lists it on Sundays at 9 a.m. Pacific. The first subtitled episode is dated October 11. If you are following both releases, use the Thursday launch for the dub and the Sunday listing for the Japanese-audio version. An episode number on Sunday’s page does not establish which English-language episode is available that day.

A weekly rollout also means viewers waiting for the dub can be behind friends watching the subtitled episodes. In episode discussions, look for the language and episode number together. Crunchyroll had already posted subtitled installments before announcing this dub. Reading the latest Japanese-audio episode synopsis can reveal developments beyond the English release.

Which countries get the dub on Crunchyroll?

The Toei Animation and Crunchyroll announcement names the United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand and Latin America for the English, Spanish and Portuguese dubs. The Japanese-audio rollout announced in October covers a broader set of markets, including Europe, India, Southeast Asia, Africa and the Middle East. If you already see the subtitled series in one of those markets, check the local audio listing for the November rollout. Its presence in the catalog establishes access to the existing version and says little about the rights to the new audio tracks.

The official Japanese site also lists distribution partners for the series, including platforms outside Crunchyroll. Use that list to find the edition in your country, then check the specific platform’s audio and release information. A series listing on Netflix, Hulu or Disney+ does not establish that its English audio arrives at the same time as Crunchyroll’s. Disney’s press office, for instance, gave Spain an October 14 Disney+ premiere. That is a regional release date for the series. The November dub announcement has a separate platform and territory list.

If you are planning to watch with someone overseas, share the official series page as well as the premiere time. Two people can be looking at the same Crunchyroll URL while seeing different language options. For the English dub specifically, use the countries named for the November release and the audio options visible on the individual episode. The broader franchise and platform announcements answer different questions.

How to find the right episode and audio track

Open the official Dragon Ball Super: Beerus series page and choose the episode there. The first episode is titled “The God Who Destroys.” The Japanese-audio episode record shows English among its subtitle options. To hear the dubbed performance, check the audio selector for English when the dub becomes available in your region. Turning on English subtitles alone leaves the Japanese dialogue in place.

Search with the subtitle Beerus included. Crunchyroll also carries the original Dragon Ball Super, which already has an English dub in supported markets. Finding an episode in that older listing is easy because the two series tell versions of the same early story and share familiar voices. Check the title above the player before relying on the audio menu. The specific 2026 series listing is the one tied to the November 12 announcement.

If the episode plays in Japanese after launch, check the episode’s audio menu first. A language setting on another device or an older viewing session can affect what the player opens with. If English is absent from the menu, compare your region with the announced territory list and check the episode itself. The announcement establishes the release window, while the catalog is the place to verify what you can actually play.

Who voices Goku, Vegeta, Beerus and Whis?

Sean Schemmel returns as Goku, Christopher R. Sabat as Vegeta, Jason Douglas as Beerus and Ian Sinclair as Whis. These four actors also voiced those characters in the original series’ English dub. Douglas and Sinclair appeared at the October New York Comic Con advance screening, which is why their names showed up in earlier coverage. That panel preview took place before the streaming launch and had a separate date.

The Spanish and Portuguese casts are separate from the English lineup. The announcement names Mario Castañeda as Goku in Spanish and Wendel Bezerra in Portuguese. If your preference is one of those tracks, choose it in the same series listing when it becomes available. All three audio releases are part of the November announcement. The cast list tells you who you hear after you select the language.

Is this the original Dragon Ball Super or a new sequel?

The original television series continued beyond this opening story into later arcs. For a viewer looking for those episodes, the Beerus subtitle is a signal to check the edition before pressing play. For a viewer who wants the remade opening, the updated listing is the relevant one even if a search for Goku and Beerus brings up the original series first.

Beerus is Toei Animation’s enhanced edition of the opening Beerus story from Dragon Ball Super. It revisits the period after Majin Buu’s defeat, when Beerus wakes and seeks out the Saiyan who defeated Frieza. Toei describes the new edition as a reconstruction with additional and redrawn material. The core encounter is familiar, while the production presents it again with changes to how the story looks and unfolds.

For a newcomer, the first episode provides a starting point for this version of the Beerus arc. If you want the complete run of the original Dragon Ball Super, use the original series listing. If you are deciding whether to revisit the opening arc, sample the dub trailer and start with the 2026 edition’s first episode. Matching the episode number across versions can be misleading because each listing has its own sequence.

Japan’s original October 11 television premiere and October 12 streaming launch on Prime Video and U-NEXT remain useful reference points for comparing the release history. The November date adds a language option for the 2026 edition on Crunchyroll. It does not reset the Japanese-audio episode count.
