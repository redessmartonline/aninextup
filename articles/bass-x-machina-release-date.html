How the frontier setting sharpens the central dilemma

Bass X Machina combines a lawless Western landscape with machines and supernatural dangers, but its family-protection premise gives those elements a personal cost. The father's decisions may protect the people closest to him while also threatening their trust in him. That tension is a useful way to approach the story without predicting events not described in the official synopsis.

When does Bass X Machina release?

Bass X Machina premieres on Netflix on November 3, 2026. Netflix's official Annecy announcement lists November 3, 2026 as the release date. Netflix Japan's worldwide launch announcement separately announces the same date for a worldwide exclusive launch, so viewers elsewhere should check their local catalog.

Where to watch

Bass X Machina is a Netflix series. Availability is tied to Netflix's regional catalogs, so AniNextUp will keep the release guide updated if the service publishes territory-specific differences. What is Bass X Machina about?

The story takes place in a lawless steampunk West populated by violent outlaws, machines and supernatural threats. A father is forced into the roles of judge, jury and executioner while trying to protect his family, even as every violent decision risks pushing those same people away.

Who is making the series?

LeSean Thomas created the series and Studio Mir handles animation. Brian Tyree Henry stars and is also an executive producer alongside Thomas, Jennifer Wiley-Moxley and Chad Handley. What the announced story and credits actually confirm

Netflix describes Bass X Machina as a series set in a lawless steampunk West, where outlaws, machines and supernatural threats surround a father trying to protect his family. The premise gives the action a conflict beyond survival: his role as judge, jury and executioner may create distance between him and the people he wants to save. That is the central tension the official synopsis offers; it does not reveal how the family responds or how the story resolves it.

LeSean Thomas created the series and Studio Mir is the animation studio. Brian Tyree Henry is both a star and an executive producer; Tati Gabrielle and Cree Summer are also in the announced cast. The executive producers listed by Netflix include Thomas, Henry, Jennifer Wiley-Moxley and Chad Handley. These credits help readers distinguish the creative roles: Thomas is the series creator, Studio Mir produces the animation, and Henry’s involvement extends beyond voice performance.

Who else appears in Netflix's cast list?

Alongside Brian Tyree Henry, Tati Gabrielle and Cree Summer, the Netflix title page lists Currie Graham, Chaske Spencer, Starletta DuPois, Nick Thurston, André Sogliuzzo, Anoli Scott and Lynette Willis. A list of performers is not necessarily a complete character guide: Netflix's public listing does not assign a role to every name.

Is the series a Japanese television anime?

Netflix describes Bass X Machina as an internationally produced animated series influenced by anime, created by LeSean Thomas and animated by Studio Mir. Its announcement places the project among Netflix's broader animation slate; it does not describe a Japanese television broadcast. The confirmed viewing plan is a Netflix launch, rather than a broadcast-station schedule.

The platform classifies the series under adult animation, science fiction, action and fantasy, reflecting its combination of Western imagery and machines. Classification and age ratings can differ by region, so viewers should check the local Netflix page for the rating and available audio before watching.

The title page distinguishes the earlier teaser from the longer Season 1 trailer. Both are promotional material for the same series, and neither confirms a separate premiere or a full episode count.

Trailer, teaser and release date are separate updates

Netflix has published an earlier teaser and a later full trailer. The two links on this page point to separate official uploads, so the teaser should not be mistaken for the newest footage. Neither video changes the release date: Netflix lists November 3, 2026 for the series, and its Anime NYC announcement describes the launch as a worldwide exclusive. A local catalog remains the final check for viewing in a specific country.

At the time of this update, Netflix has not posted an exact release hour or a detailed episode rollout in the cited announcements. AniNextUp therefore lists the confirmed day and leaves the hour open. The phrase “worldwide” describes Netflix’s announced launch plan; it does not tell viewers what time the title becomes playable in every time zone or whether every language track is ready at the same moment.
