When did PSYREN start, and where can you watch?

PSYREN premiered on October 5, 2026. Crunchyroll's release announcement places the subtitled debut at 7:30 a.m. Pacific Time, with availability worldwide excluding Japan and China. That was the opening episode's announced streaming time; it should not be read as a promise covering every later episode, language track or service.

For the premiere date, 7:30 a.m. Pacific was 14:30 UTC: 10:30 a.m. Eastern Daylight Time, 10:30 a.m. in Santo Domingo and 3:30 p.m. British Summer Time. These are conversions of the October 5 announcement. Daylight-saving changes can alter later local-clock equivalents, so check your regional episode listing before planning a weekly reminder.

Why the Japanese schedule shows a different time

The official Japanese schedule lists TOKYO MX and BS11 on Mondays at 23:00 JST from October 5. Sun TV lists Monday at 24:00, meaning midnight at the start of Tuesday. Mie TV begins October 7 at 24:20, meaning October 8 at 00:20 JST. Japanese late-night notation preserves the broadcaster's programming day; your calendar may display the following date. Japanese streaming begins from October 5 at 23:30 JST, with exceptions for some services. The schedule includes ABEMA, Prime Video, Hulu and U-NEXT among its subscription options. Those Japanese licenses do not establish international availability on the same brands. Use Crunchyroll's territorial announcement for its simulcast and the Japanese schedule for domestic distribution.

What is PSYREN about?

The official premise starts with first-year high-school student Ageha Yoshina finding a red telephone card in a public phone booth. His childhood friend Sakurako Amamiya has a matching card, then disappears. Searching for her draws Ageha toward Psyren, a rumored secret society linked to disappearances across Japan, and into a game with lethal stakes.

This is a useful starting point for viewers choosing between a battle series and a mystery: the rescue gives Ageha a personal reason to enter, while the cards and disappearances provide questions beyond who wins a fight. That is our reading of the published setup, rather than a verdict on the full adaptation. The premise does not reveal the game's answers, and this guide avoids them. Do you need to read the manga first?

No preparatory reading is necessary to understand the announced introduction. The anime starts with Ageha's discovery, making Episode 1 the practical entry point. If you prefer reading, VIZ's official Psyren page identifies Toshiaki Iwashiro as the creator and offers a Volume 1 preview. It describes a dangerous alternate dimension and powers that complicate Ageha's understanding of both worlds.

The manga is complete: VIZ labels Volume 16 as the final volume. Starting at Volume 1 lets you follow its clues in order. Avoid jumping to the last book's synopsis if you want the central mystery preserved. A completed source series does not by itself confirm how much a television season adapts.

Who makes the anime and voices the characters?

The official production credits name Satelight as animation studio, Katsumi Ono as director, Shin Yoshida for series composition and Akira Okuma for character design. Yoshitaka Taniguchi designs the creatures. Takashi Ohmama, Tatsuhiko Saiki and Shu Kanematsu compose the music. Related reading: The Vermilion Mask Release Date, Time & Where to Watch.

Rikuya Yasuda voices Ageha, with Mayuko Kazama as Sakurako. Shunsuke Takeuchi plays Hiryu Asaga, Soma Saito plays Oboro Mochizuki and Yukihiro Nozuyama plays Kabuto Kirisaki. The cast also includes Yuhko Kaida as Matsuri Yagumo and Nobuyuki Hiyama as Dolkey. These are Japanese cast credits; they are not an English-dub lineup.

What are the opening and ending songs?

The official music page identifies CLAN QUEEN's “WARNING!!” as the first-cour opening and Who-ya Extended's “ICARUS” as its ending. A first-cour song credit is not an episode-total announcement. Opening and ending videos are also separate promotional uploads from a teaser or story trailer.
