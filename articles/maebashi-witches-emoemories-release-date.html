When did the Maebashi Witches movie release?

Maebashi Witches – Emoemories: Blooming Witches opened in Japanese theaters on October 23, 2026. That date has passed; the film is currently in its Japanese theatrical run, with showtimes varying by cinema and day. The official theater listing publishes a rolling schedule, so check the local venue before traveling rather than relying on a single nationwide start time.

The official project site has not announced an overseas theatrical date or a streaming service for this film. Its October 23 release is specifically the Japanese cinema opening. A Japanese premiere does not establish a later date for the United States, Europe, Latin America, or any other territory.

Is it a recap, a sequel, or a new movie?

The theatrical edition rebuilds the original 12-episode TV anime into a feature lasting just under two hours. The official description calls it a reconstruction of the series, but also stresses that it is not simply a compilation: the film adds new episodes or story passages, new Witchverse scenes, songs, newly recorded material, and additional dialogue. Director Shunichi Yamamoto says the production reshaped the order and direction while assembling the story into a film-length experience. Related reading: Aikatsu Stars! That makes this a theatrical re-edit with new material, not an announced second season or a separate sequel. The official October 16 update released six new stills and comments from Yamamoto and series composer and screenwriter Erika Yoshida. The announcements do not give a count of every new scene or a scene-by-scene comparison with the 12 TV episodes, so viewers should not treat a specific “minutes of new footage” figure as confirmed.

Can you watch the movie without seeing the TV anime?

Yes, the director presents the film as accessible to people who only know the title or are meeting the series for the first time. It condenses the central story into a single visit of roughly two hours, which is a practical entry point for someone who has not watched all 12 episodes. The official comments also say the restructured presentation and new scenes give returning viewers fresh material to notice.

What is the story about?

The story is set in Maebashi, Gunma Prefecture, and follows ordinary high-school student Yuina Akagi after she meets Az, Kyoka, Choco, and Mai. The five girls begin witch training while working at a magical flower shop, where they face customers’ wishes and try to help the “flowers” in people’s hearts bloom. Keroppe, the frog-like companion, is also part of the group’s story. Related reading: The Apothecary Diaries Movie: Luoqing and Yinshan Character PV.

Yoshida frames the story around the effort involved in both maintaining the status quo and choosing to move forward. The girls make mistakes, respond differently to the wishes they encounter, and build solidarity through their choices; those emotional decisions connect the episodic premise into a feature-length story.

Who voices the Maebashi Witches?

The main cast returns for the theatrical edition: Sakura Kasuga voices Yuina Akagi, Hinano Sakikawa voices Az Niizato, Rena Motomura voices Kyoka Kitahara, Haruka Minami voices Choco Mimata, and Honami Momose voices Mai Kamiizumi. Tomokazu Sugita voices Keroppe. These names are confirmed on the official series cast page.

Sunrise is credited with the original work and production. Yamamoto directs, Yoshida handles series composition and script, and Bandai Namco Filmworks is credited for production. The official October 16 comments note that the new script passages and added lines were developed with the film edit, which distinguishes the theatrical work from a simple episode-to-episode splice. Is there a first-week theater bonus?

The official first-week campaign runs October 23–29, 2026, and offers one of five limited mini shikishi designs illustrated by character designer Nozomi Tachibana. The gift is distributed at participating Japanese theaters while supplies last; the date range alone does not guarantee that a particular cinema still has copies. Contact the theater or check its notice before buying a ticket if the bonus matters to your visit.
