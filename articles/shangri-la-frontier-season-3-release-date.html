Shangri-La Frontier Season 3 Release Date: January 10, 2027

When does Shangri-La Frontier Season 3 come out?

Shangri-La Frontier Season 3 premieres in Japan on January 10, 2027. The official broadcast page lists MBS and TBS's nationwide 28-station network, with new episodes on Sundays at 5:00 p.m. Japan Standard Time (JST). The production announced the specific date, weekly slot, and two-cour plan on September 16, 2026. The schedule may still change, so the official on-air page is the best place to check for a later notice.

That 5:00 p.m. time is the Japanese television slot. It is not an international streaming timestamp. Viewers outside Japan should avoid converting it into a local simulcast time until a distributor posts a schedule for their region.

What does “two consecutive cours” mean?

The production says Season 3 will run for two consecutive cours. In Japanese television scheduling, a cour is a programming block of roughly three months, so the announcement signals a continuous run across two blocks rather than a short special. It does not announce an exact episode count, guarantee that every week will have an episode, or state the finale date. Those details remain unconfirmed in the official schedule.

The January 10 start also gives fans a practical planning answer: the Japanese broadcast is a Sunday evening program, not a January release with a day still to be announced. If you are tracking the season from another country, keep the date and the local streaming time as separate facts.

It lists streaming services for Seasons 1 and 2, which can help returning viewers catch up in Japan; those lists do not prove that Season 3 will be available on every service or in every country. A global platform, regional catalog, subtitle launch, and dubbed release should be treated as separate announcements.

For viewers asking whether Season 3 will stream on Crunchyroll, this page does not confirm a service or simulcast time from a directly verified distributor notice. What is the new season about?

The official introduction moves the story from the game Shangri-La Frontier to the Global Game Competition. Sunraku takes a short break from his main game after a run of major encounters, including the unique monster Lycagon and the defeat of Wethermon. Oikatzo invites him to compete, and Pencilgon joins the effort as they enter Galaxia Heroes: Chaos, a new virtual-reality fighting game built on the Shangri-La Frontier engine.

The immediate challenge is a match against Star Rain, described by the official site as a leading American professional fighting-game team. This setup explains why the next arc looks different from the earlier monster hunts: Sunraku and his friends have to apply their game knowledge in a competitive arena, learn a new game's rules, and face experienced opponents. The official synopsis names this direction, but it does not reveal every match result or how much of the tournament the season will adapt.

For anyone returning after Season 2, the useful point is that the GGC is a continuation of the same characters' story, not a separate spin-off. Sunraku, Oikatzo, and Pencilgon are still central; the change is the game they are playing and the opponents in front of them. The teaser and new character material promote that transition without confirming a full episode-by-episode outline.

Who is returning, and which studio is animating it?

C2C is the animation studio. The official staff page lists Toshiyuki Kubooka as chief director, Hiro Oki as director, Kazuyuki Fudeyasu for series composition and scripts, and Ayumi Kurashima for character design and chief animation direction. The credits also include action and effects direction by Satoshi Sakai and music by Ryōichi Takada, Kōhei Hirokawa, and Kuniyuki Takahashi of MONACA.

Yuma Uchida returns as Sunraku/Rakurō, Azumi Waki as Saiga-0/Rei, Yōko Hikasa as Arthur Pencilgon/Towa, and Makoto Koichi as Oikatzo/Kei. The official cast list also names Yumi Uchiyama as Natsume Megumi, Minami Takahashi as Silvia Goldberg, and Daiki Hamano as Lucas Garcia. Those credits make the competition arc's new opponents easier to follow, while the returning trio keeps the series connected to its earlier seasons.
