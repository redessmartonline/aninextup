Multi-Mind Mayhem Anime Release Date: Episode 1 & 2 Schedule, Trailer and Where to Watch

When do Multi-Mind Mayhem episodes 1 and 2 come out?

Multi-Mind Mayhem premieres Wednesday, January 6, 2027, at 10:00 p.m. JST on TOKYO MX. The anime’s official schedule lists episodes 1 and 2 together on d Anime Store, ABEMA and U-NEXT at 10:30 p.m. JST on the same day. This is a two-episode launch for those three Japanese streaming services. The 10:00 p.m. time is the start of the TOKYO MX broadcast.

Japanese schedules often use times past 23:59 to keep an after-midnight program with the previous evening’s listings. In this case, BS Fuji’s January 6 at 24:00 means midnight at the start of January 7. TOKYO MX is listed for Wednesdays at 10:00 p.m., BS Fuji for Wednesdays at 24:00, and AT-X for Fridays at 8:30 p.m. The official broadcast page also lists AT-X repeats and notes that times can change.

For comparisons across time zones, the streaming launch at 10:30 p.m. JST converts to 13:30 UTC, 8:30 a.m. Eastern in the United States and 9:30 a.m. in Santo Domingo on January 6. These conversions describe the Japanese release. Access in any other country depends on its own licensed service and schedule.

Why are two episodes available on the first day?

The production explicitly scheduled both episodes for the first streaming date on the three named services. The announcement describes the arrangement as an advance subscription stream ahead of terrestrial television. That is why someone looking at the first TOKYO MX slot and someone looking at the streaming schedule can both see January 6 yet have a different number of episodes available to them.

If you are choosing where to watch in Japan, start with the type of access you have. Viewers using one of the three named services can look for both opening episodes after 10:30 p.m. JST. Viewers following a TV station should use that station’s schedule and episode listing. The official notice specifically names episodes 1 and 2 for launch, so later dates should be checked as individual listings appear.

What is Multi-Mind Mayhem about?

Bard Cornelius is a young noble in the Kingdom of Mauricia. He shares his body with the souls of Oka Sanai, a warrior from Japan’s Sengoku era, and Masaharu Oka, a modern high school student. Bard can draw on their experience as he learns to manage land, deal with rivals and test his abilities in combat. The unusual combination makes the same problem look different depending on whose knowledge is relevant.

That setup gives the show a practical side alongside the battles. A warrior can help Bard think about tactics, while someone from modern Japan brings a different set of ideas to trade and invention. Bard’s position gives him a reason to try those ideas in his own world. If you are deciding whether to start the books, that mix of economics, territory and action is a better guide to the story than the reincarnation label alone.

Who voices the three characters, and who makes the anime?

Yusuke Kobayashi voices Bard Cornelius, Takashi Matsuyama voices Oka Sanai and Satoshi Inomata voices Masaharu Oka. Hiroaki Sakurai directs at Studio DEEN, with Keiichiro Ochi handling series composition. The November update also adds six cast members. Among them are Marina Inoue as Maggot, Takehito Koyasu as Ignis and Toshiki Kumagai as Brooks. The production credits the original novels to Ryousen Takami, with illustrations by Ririnra, and credits Honoji for the manga.

Where can you read the Multi-Mind Mayhem manga in English?

Start with the first chapter on Alpha Manga or Volume 1 in paperback from One Peace Books. The English paperback ISBN is 9781642731408. Searching by that number helps distinguish the print manga from Japanese editions, digital listings and the original novels. Both official English options listed here are for Honoji’s manga adaptation.

Japan’s Volume 15 announcement is a separate publication date. AlphaPolis has scheduled that Japanese manga volume for December 14, 2026. The January anime announcement does not specify a chapter cutoff or a volume to resume from after the opening episodes. Readers who want the beginning of Bard’s story can start at Volume 1, while viewers who want to come in fresh can use the trailer and keep the manga link for later.

You may find the franchise under Isekai Tensei Soudouki or 異世界転生騒動記 in Japanese listings. Add “anime” to search for the January schedule and “Honoji” to find the manga. The title spans novels, comics and the animated adaptation, so the creator credit and format are useful ways to check which edition a page is offering.

What should you check before the premiere?

For the Japanese double premiere, look for the individual episode numbers on the app you already use. A series landing page can appear before either episode is playable. The release notice names d Anime Store, ABEMA and U-NEXT for the January 6 advance stream. The broadcaster list is useful for a different purpose: it tells you which channel to tune to and which day to check, especially if you plan to follow BS Fuji’s late-night slot.

If you are checking from abroad, search for the English title and its Japanese romanization in the catalog for your country. Look for the episode listing, supported subtitle languages and a local playback date before making plans around the Japanese timetable. The strongest evidence is a release notice that names your territory or a service page that lists the episode in your region. A Japanese broadcast time alone only answers when it airs in Japan.

For the manga, the digital first chapter gives you a quick way to sample Honoji’s version of Bard’s story. If you choose print, check the language and volume number against One Peace Books’ listing. That is especially useful around the December release in Japan, when searches for the series can bring up the new Japanese volume alongside English editions. Readers deciding where to start can use the English Volume 1 listing and return to the anime schedule when the January launch gets closer.
