What is the anime about?

Rein is an Enchanter whose support magic strengthens weapons and armor. After his guild decides his work is no longer necessary and fires him, he recalls the enchantments he previously placed on their equipment and takes those accumulated bonus points back for himself.

That unusual mechanic transforms a discarded support character into someone with enormous potential. Rather than joining another elite party immediately, Rein begins again with basic clothes and a copper sword, turning the premise into a second-chance fantasy adventure.

The mechanic behind Rein’s second chance

The official series description makes Rein’s support role central to the setup. He is an Enchanter whose work strengthens the weapons and armor of his guildmates. When the party dismisses him, he remembers that those enchantments have accumulated bonus points and takes them back. That premise ties his comeback to work the guild had already benefited from, rather than a sudden unrelated power that appears after he is fired.

It is also why “support mage” is a more useful description than calling Rein weak. His contribution was embedded in the party’s equipment; the guild’s mistake was treating a less visible role as disposable. The story turns that imbalance into a fantasy progression hook. This is a reading of the published premise, not a promise that the series will provide a real-world blueprint for workplace disputes.

Who voices Rein in the English dub?

Crunchyroll's episode-one dub announcement credits Justin Briner as Rein, Hollis Beck as Lily, Lacey Deline as Nina, Kyleigh Zimmerer as Elsie and Rick Robertson as Barrius. These are the English-language credits announced for the first episode; they should not be confused with the Japanese cast.

Which version of the story inspired the anime?

The production website credits Asa Rokushima's novel, Gyomuyomochi's manga and kisui's original character concepts. It presents the animation as an adaptation of the manga and notes that the novel and manga differ substantially, so readers should not assume they tell every event in the same way.
