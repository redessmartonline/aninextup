Now That We Draw Anime Sets January 2027 Premiere

The TV adaptation of Kakunaru Ue wa begins in January 2027. Its official website has revealed the first promotional video, character visuals and two lead cast members; an exact day and streaming platform have not been announced.

When does Now That We Draw premiere?

Now That We Draw is scheduled to begin in January 2027. The official anime website confirms the month, but it does not name an exact premiere day, Japanese TV station, broadcast time or international streaming service. Readers can treat January as the current release window, not as a date for a specific episode. AniNextUp will update the guide if the production committee publishes a timetable.

The series is based on the manga Kakunaru Ue wa. The September 2026 promotion added a first PV and character visuals to the earlier adaptation announcement, giving viewers a better sense of the leads and production team. It still does not answer the day-to-day schedule or regional streaming question.

What is the story about?

Yuki Uehara dreams of becoming a manga artist and loves two-dimensional characters. After an editor criticizes his romantic-comedy manga for lacking realistic romance, he considers giving up. Classmate Niina Miyamoto discovers his work and reveals that she also wants to become a manga artist. The pair start a “secret pretend relationship” to help with their manga, setting up a youth romantic comedy about two aspiring creators.

The creative goal matters to the premise: both students are trying to write romance while admitting that they have little personal experience with it. Their arrangement gives them a reason to observe how people behave together, but the official synopsis does not promise that the fake relationship will end in a particular way. That distinction is useful for readers looking for a spoiler-free introduction: the confirmed hook is shared ambition and an unusual research pact, not a stated final couple outcome. The original manga is written by Kyū Takahata and illustrated by Yūji Kaba; it is serialized in Hakusensha’s Young Animal. The anime website and the production company’s published series information identify those creators and the magazine.

Cast, staff and music

Ayumu Murase voices Yuki Uehara, while Sayumi Suzushiro voices Niina Miyamoto. The official site describes Yuki as an inward-looking high-school student who cares deeply about his interests; Niina is an energetic, popular classmate who shares his manga ambition. These profiles explain why the characters’ creative partnership is central to the setup.

ROLL2 is producing the animation. Shunsuke Ishikawa directs, Deko Akao handles series composition and scripts, and Hiromi Nakagawa designs the characters. The official staff list also names sub-character design, color design, art, photography, 3D, editing, sound and music credits. shallm performs the opening theme “Kimiiro Hirari,” announced by production company Infinite on October 8, 2026. No ending song has been named in the sources reviewed.

Can readers start with the manga?

Yes. The original manga is available in English from Seven Seas Entertainment under the title Now That We Draw, also listed with its Japanese title Kakunaru Ue wa. Its official catalogue describes both leads as high-school manga creators and lists English volumes 1–5 as released, with volume 6 scheduled for December 22, 2026 and volume 7 for May 18, 2027. Those are book-release dates from the English publisher; they are not anime episode dates.

Starting with the manga is a practical option for readers who want to meet Yuki and Niina before the broadcast. It does not establish how many chapters the anime will adapt, whether the adaptation will cover a complete story arc, or whether it will make changes. The anime’s episode count and source-material endpoint have not been announced, so chapter-to-episode estimates would be speculation.

Where to watch and what remains unknown

The Japanese broadcast month is confirmed, but the network and streaming outlet have not been announced on the official anime site. No international license or territory list is published in the official sources reviewed. A platform that carries the manga, or streams other anime by the same studio, is not evidence that it will carry this series. Readers outside Japan should wait for a distributor announcement before planning a subscription or expecting same-day episodes.
