Now That We Draw Anime Sets January 2027 Premiere

The TV adaptation of Kakunaru Ue wa begins in January 2027. Its official website has revealed the first promotional video, character visuals and two lead cast members; an exact day and streaming platform have not been announced.

When does Now That We Draw premiere?

Now That We Draw is scheduled to begin in January 2027. The official anime website confirms the month, but it does not list an exact broadcast date, time or television network. The site also does not name an international streaming service, so platform availability remains unconfirmed.

The series is based on the manga Kakunaru Ue wa. Its first promotional video and character materials were unveiled alongside the January broadcast window.

What is the story about?

Yuki Uehara dreams of becoming a manga artist and loves two-dimensional characters. After an editor criticizes his romantic comedy manga for lacking realistic romance, he considers giving up. Classmate Niina Miyamoto discovers his work and reveals that she also wants to become a manga artist. The pair start a “secret pretend relationship” to help with their manga, setting up a youth romantic comedy about two aspiring creators. The original manga is written by Kyū Takahata and illustrated by Yūji Kaba; it is serialized in Hakusensha’s Young Animal. Those source credits are listed on the official anime site.

Cast and staff

Ayumu Murase voices Yuki Uehara, while Sayumi Suzushiro voices Niina Miyamoto. ROLL2 is producing the animation. Shunsuke Ishikawa directs, Deco Akao handles series composition and scripts, and Hiromi Nakagawa designs the characters. The official site lists “Kimiiro Hirari” by shallm as the opening theme.

What the first PV establishes for the adaptation

The official site gives readers a useful dividing line between what is known and what is still marketing language. The anime adapts Kyū Takahata and Yūji Kaba’s manga, published in Hakusensha’s Young Animal. Its central setup is not simply “two classmates fall in love”: Yuki wants to draw romantic comedy, Niina wants to become a manga artist too, and their pretend relationship is a practical arrangement that feeds their work. That makes the creative partnership part of the premise, not just a backdrop.

That distinction gives the first PV more context. The video and character materials introduce the leads and the show’s romantic-comedy premise, while the official announcement confirms the January 2027 broadcast window and the principal cast and staff. It does not establish a particular January premiere day, a Japanese network, or an international streaming service. Viewers should treat those as open questions until the production publishes a schedule.

Why the creators’ premise matters

A story about people trying to write romance can play with the gap between what a character thinks is romantic and what another person actually feels. In this case, that is an interpretation of the announced setup, not a confirmed plot summary of later episodes. It is a more useful expectation than promising a specific romantic outcome the official materials have not disclosed.

For viewers deciding whether to follow the series, the confirmed staff list is also practical: ROLL2 handles animation, Shunsuke Ishikawa directs, Deco Akao oversees series composition and scripts, and Hiromi Nakagawa designs the characters.
