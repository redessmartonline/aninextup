BLESS Anime Release Date, Crunchyroll Streaming & Manga Guide

The BLESS anime premieres January 8, 2027, at 11:30 p.m. JST on Fuji TV’s noitaminA block in Japan. Crunchyroll has confirmed January streaming worldwide excluding Japan and China. Watch the first full trailer below and find the official English manga edition if you want to start reading before the premiere.

When does BLESS premiere?

BLESS premieres in Japan on Friday, January 8, 2027, at 11:30 p.m. JST. The production lists the series on Fuji TV’s nationwide noitaminA block, with broadcasts scheduled for Fridays at that time. The November 7 update gives an exact day and time for a premiere that had previously been listed only for January. The local broadcast time converts to 9:30 a.m. Eastern and 6:30 a.m. Pacific on January 8. Those conversions describe when the Japanese broadcast begins.

For anyone setting a reminder outside Japan, the important difference is between the time on the Japanese TV schedule and the time an episode appears on a streaming service. Use January 8 for the Japanese premiere. For a local viewing reminder, use the time displayed by Crunchyroll for your territory once the platform publishes its episode schedule. The weekly Japanese slot is a reference for the broadcast rather than a guarantee that every streaming episode will be available at that minute.

Where to watch BLESS on Crunchyroll

Crunchyroll announced January 2027 streaming worldwide excluding Japan and China. That announced territory includes the United States, Canada and the United Kingdom, as well as Latin America. The platform announcement reviewed for this update gives the month, while the Japanese production gives the date and time for Fuji TV. The regional Crunchyroll episode listing will be the place to confirm the exact streaming time.

An English dub date, a list of subtitle languages and the total episode count were absent from the announcements reviewed for this guide. If English audio is the reason you are waiting, look for a specific dub announcement or an English audio option on the episode page. A streaming announcement can establish which service carries a show months before the service publishes those language details.

There is also a separate live-action adaptation reported for Prime Video in spring 2027. That listing concerns another version of Sonoyama’s manga. If you are looking for the A-1 Pictures anime or the scenes in the trailer below, follow the Crunchyroll announcement or the noitaminA broadcast schedule in Japan.

What the first full BLESS trailer reveals

Aniplex released the first full promotional video on November 7. It uses footage from the series, gives the Japanese broadcast date, and previews HIKKA’s ending theme, New Page. Earlier videos focused on individual characters, including Akiharu Nakano. This trailer is the broader introduction to the adaptation. The main visual released alongside it uses the shape and colors of a makeup palette to frame Aia and Jun in two separate spaces, with objects around them that refer to other members of the cast.

Watch the official trailer on YouTube

The trailer is a useful introduction if the name alone gives little away. Look for the difference between what each lead does in front of other people and what each wants to learn. Aia has already been looked at as a model, yet he wants to work with makeup. Jun is the one who wants to be in front of an audience, even though she is hesitant about being seen. That exchange of roles explains why their partnership matters more than the result of a single runway contest.

What is BLESS about?

Aia Udagawa has experience in front of the camera and a private interest in becoming a makeup artist. Jun Sumisaki is a student who wants to model and has learned to draw as little attention to herself as possible. When they team up for a school fashion contest, Aia can work behind the scenes while Jun takes the runway. They have to trust each other with skills they are still learning and with ambitions they have had trouble saying aloud.

The setup gives each character a practical problem as well as an emotional one. Aia needs to develop technique and decide what he wants his work to communicate. Jun needs to find a way to be visible on her own terms. Makeup can change how other people see her, but the story’s interest lies in the decisions the two make together, including what parts of herself Jun chooses to highlight. The contest gives those decisions a deadline and an audience.

Cast and the earlier Akiharu Nakano reveal

Tomohiro Ono voices Aia and Chiyuki Miura voices Jun. Koki Uchiyama voices Akiharu Nakano, whose own character preview was published in October. Nakano is a student associated with Mirror, where aspiring makeup artists train. He is reserved and technically quick, and he looks up to Hikaru Oya, voiced by Tarusuke Shingaki. Uchiyama’s casting was the focus of this guide when it first appeared. Watch Aniplex’s earlier Nakano character preview.

Shotaro Kitamura directs at A-1 Pictures, with Takayuki Kikuchi as assistant director. Fukuro Kamiza handles series composition, Honoka Yokoyama designs the characters, and Ryo Yoshimata composes the score. The production also credits hair and makeup supervision. That is a role worth noticing in a story where a character’s choice of tools and the sequence of their work can be part of the drama, even when there is very little dialogue.

Where to read the BLESS manga in English

Start with Kodansha’s English edition of Bless, Volume 1, by Yukino Sonoyama. Kodansha lists print and digital editions, and its series page offers a free chapter preview. If you are ordering through a bookstore, the print ISBN is 9798888771792. Use the volume number and author together when searching, since the short English title can also bring up unrelated books and shows.

Volume 1 gives you the school contest and the beginning of Aia and Jun’s collaboration. It is the place to start even if you came to the series through the Nakano casting video. The next volume is where Kodansha’s description introduces the training environment at Mirror and follows Jun’s early attempts to get modeling work. Those are useful landmarks for choosing what to read before January. The production has yet to announce a volume-by-volume plan for the season, so the manga’s numbering is a better reading guide than guesses about episode coverage.

You can sample the chapter first to see how the manga presents its fashion sequences on the page. If you are waiting for the anime and want to avoid later developments, the first volume introduces the leads and their project without requiring you to read ahead into their separate career paths. If your priority is watching episode one as it becomes available, the official Crunchyroll schedule for your region is the source to check as January approaches.

Which version of BLESS should you start with?

If you are curious about the premise and want to start now, the English manga is already available. The print and digital versions of Volume 1 give you the same starting point, so choose based on how you prefer to read rather than looking for a separate anime tie-in edition. The official preview is also a quick way to make sure you have the right Bless before buying it.

If you prefer to experience the runway scenes with voice acting and music, start with the full trailer, then look for Episode 1 in January. The trailer already gives you a sample of how the series presents makeup as a craft: it can move between what the characters are doing with their hands and what another person sees from across the room. Readers who found the series through an actor can still use the earlier character preview to hear that performance in isolation before watching the broader footage.
