d Anime Store Announces 81 Anime Titles for Fall 2026 in Japan

Japanese anime streaming service d Anime Store says its fall 2026 lineup has reached 81 titles. The figure is a snapshot of the service's Japan-focused catalog, not a worldwide streaming announcement.

What did d Anime Store announce?

On October 13, 2026, Japan's d Anime Store announced that its autumn anime streaming selection totaled 81 titles. The number describes its fall-season offering at the time of the announcement. It should not be interpreted as 81 brand-new productions, 81 global premieres, or 81 exclusives.

Does this mean 81 exclusive anime?

No. A lineup count is different from an exclusive-title count. The service's fall selection can include simulcast series, catalog availability, and titles with different release arrangements. Viewers should consult each title's listing for exact start times, subscription conditions and any early or exclusive distribution notes.

Can viewers outside Japan watch these anime?

This is a Japan-specific streaming announcement. It does not confirm that the same 81 titles are available internationally, nor does it establish distribution rights on Crunchyroll, Netflix, Disney+ or other services. Regional availability varies by title and must be checked separately against each platform's official listings.

How to check the full fall 2026 lineup

The most reliable way to identify individual shows and their streaming conditions is to consult the official d Anime Store seasonal lineup and title pages. Because schedules and licensing conditions may change, AniNextUp is not reproducing an unverified title-by-title list here.

Why individual streaming labels matter

The official autumn lineup distinguishes ordinary streaming listings from entries marked as exclusive, early access or rental. Those labels can affect when a subscriber can watch a particular episode and whether an additional charge applies. The headline total of 81 titles does not mean every listed show has identical rights or availability conditions.

What this means for anime fans

The announcement offers a useful snapshot of the breadth of the fall season on one Japanese service. For viewers deciding where to watch, the more important questions remain which specific series are available in their country, when each episode appears and whether a title is included in their subscription.

What do the Japanese timing labels mean?

A title marked before terrestrial broadcast is scheduled on the service ahead of its Japanese TV airing. A simultaneous label means it is scheduled at the same time as terrestrial broadcast, while an earliest subscription label compares its timing with other subscription services. A listing may combine more than one of those descriptions.

Dates and hours are attached to individual shows, not to the entire seasonal catalog. Japanese schedules can also use a clock beyond 24:00 for late-night programming; for example, 26:00 refers to 2:00 a.m. on the following calendar day in Japan. Check the current title page and the stated time zone before converting an episode time.

How the lineup has changed since the announcement

The official notice has continued to add titles after the original count. Its current update names Tanuki and Fox, Horror Collector, Dragon Ball Super: Beerus, Punirunes Punii 4 and Reincarnated as a Sword II. That is a useful reminder that a seasonal lineup is a changing catalog snapshot, not a permanent total. The service’s notice page is the source to consult for the latest additions; check each title’s page for its own release timing.

How to check access and release conditions

d Anime Store is a Japan-focused service. This announcement therefore cannot be used as proof that the same catalog is available on international services or that a viewer outside Japan can subscribe and play every listed show. Search by the Japanese or official localized title in the relevant service’s own catalog, and confirm whether the listing is included in a flat-rate plan, rental-only or marked for early/exclusive distribution.

Those labels answer different questions. “Exclusive” is a rights description for a specific distribution window or service set; “early” indicates timing relative to other platforms; “rental” can involve a separate payment. A large headline count hides these differences. AniNextUp’s guide is meant to help readers understand the announcement and find the official listing, not promise access that the source does not establish.
