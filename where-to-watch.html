How to check where an anime streams in your country

Choose a platform above to find AniNextUp’s release guides. Each listing shows the platform information and date we have for that title. Open the guide to see what the announcement covers, then search the distributor’s catalog while signed in from your country. The catalog is where you can confirm which season, episodes, subtitles and audio tracks you can actually play.

  • Crunchyroll
  • Netflix
  • Disney+
  • Prime Video
  • HIDIVE

Start with the exact season or film

Search the series name, then open the season selector. A platform can carry the first season while a new season appears under a different listing or on a different service. If a franchise has a recap film, a theatrical sequel and a television season with similar names, compare the subtitle, year and format before relying on any date. For a continuation labeled Part 2 or “second cour,” check the episode numbers as well. The app may place those episodes inside the season you already started.

Try the English and Japanese titles when the first search returns nothing. Use the publisher or studio announcement to find other official titles or spellings. An app result for a trailer, recap or older adaptation can look like a match in search. Opening the episode list gives you a quick way to identify the release you came for.

Read the territory next to the platform

A date that names Japan is useful for following the Japanese premiere. It does not establish when the same episode can be played in the Dominican Republic, the United States or elsewhere. When a guide says “select regions,” look for a country list from the distributor and confirm that your local catalog offers the title. Crunchyroll’s help center explains how regional licenses work.

If a title vanishes while you are traveling, check the service’s country guidance before treating it as a cancellation. Prime Video documents how its selection can change away from home. Netflix lists several additional reasons a title may be missing, including a profile’s language or maturity settings. Its help page can save you time when another person in the same household sees a different result. Check Netflix’s missing-title guide.

Check the language options on the episode

An English title in the app identifies the show. To confirm an English dub, open the episode’s audio and subtitle controls and look for English in the audio list. Check the subtitle menu separately if you want Japanese audio with English captions. When only the newest episode is missing a track you use, compare it with the previous episode. The guide for the particular show can help you see whether the distributor announced a separate dub schedule.

For a family watching together, test the first playable episode on the device you plan to use. An announcement may identify a language without showing which episodes carry it on launch day. The controls on that episode give the most direct answer. If an English dub matters, verify the track before buying a season or starting a subscription for that release.

Compare the dates before setting a reminder

A title can have several valid dates: its Japanese TV premiere, a streaming debut in another country, a later English dub, or a movie screening. Choose the date tied to the version and region you intend to watch. When we give a time conversion from Japan, read the label to check whether it describes the original broadcast or a confirmed international stream. The same calendar date can represent different local release windows.

For upcoming titles, use the anime release calendar to compare dates. For the next few days, use This Week and open the individual guide if timing matters. When a platform gives a window such as “January 2027,” revisit the guide or the service listing as the month approaches. An exact day is useful only once the distributor or production has tied it to that release.

Find out what access the service offers

On a title page, look at the playback action for the exact season or movie. A subscription, an add-on channel, a rental and a digital purchase can lead to different access options. Check the price, episode selection and language information shown to your account before paying. If a page points to a distributor’s trailer instead, use it to preview the series and check the service separately for full episodes.

For a film announced only in theaters, check the distributor’s country-specific release page and local cinema listings. The film guide may list a screening date or festival showing weeks before a wider theatrical opening. That information helps you choose a screening, and a streaming date becomes useful when the distributor announces a separate home release.

What to do when an anime has no confirmed service

Look for the latest production announcement, the series page and the distributor’s announcement for your country. A publisher’s trailer, a novel listing or a Japanese TV schedule can confirm that the anime exists and tell you when to follow the next update. For playback, look for a named service, a covered territory and an episode or film listing. On AniNextUp, a title guide may remain useful even if it can only answer the release window, the cast and the source material for now.

Suppose a new show has a Japanese date and you want to watch in Santo Domingo. Check the regional note on the guide. Then search the relevant official catalog in the Dominican Republic and confirm the title, season and available languages there. If you find only an earlier season or the trailer, save the guide and check the next distributor update. This keeps your reminder tied to the release you can use.

If a catalog changes or you find an official announcement that updates one of our listings, share the title, country and official link with AniNextUp. That combination lets us verify the correction for readers in the same territory.
